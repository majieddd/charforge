Moves from generated video, matched to the video

What got built today, how it works, and every number behind it: a character's reference image becomes a video of it doing a move (MiniMax H3), the video becomes a clip on the character's game rig, and Qwen-Image 2.1 was tested against Krea 2 as the model that draws the reference. Then the goal changed to making the character move exactly as the video does: measured frame by frame against the video, the rig's pose error came down 34-56% and its silhouette overlap rose from 0.61-0.75 to 0.77-0.84. The hands, which the pose model never reached, now follow DWPose's reading of them: Aoi's palms face the camera where the video's do.

Workflow

Two lanes, one skeleton

A character is built once from a prompt or an image. Moves are then added to it from videos - one video per move serves every character, because the clip it becomes is a standard Mixamo skeleton.

Making a character charforge.py make · ~40 min

  1. Reference imageQwen-Image 2.1 (the default now) draws the character in an A-pose, cut out with its own alpha; TRELLIS takes the cut-out as it is--image-model qwen21
  2. 3D modelTRELLIS.2 turns the reference into a mesh; a second pass sees repainted side and back viewsgenerate · multiview
  3. Solid, joints, handsclosed into a solid, limbs traced to their tips, modelled hands with 15 finger bones eachsolidify · joints · hands
  4. Mesh and texturegame topology, the reference projected back on, the face from a detailed close-upretopo · texture
  5. Rig and faceweights measured through the body, head rigid to the jaw, a face rig with jaw and five shapesweights · rig · face
  6. 18 standard clipsMixamo captures retargeted with planted feet; glTF + FBX with LODsanimate · package

Adding a move from video charforge.py move · ~40 min each

  1. Write the movetimed beats - ready, the move, recovery - in MiniMax's six-section prompt formatmotion_prompts.json
  2. Generate the videoMiniMax H3 in ComfyUI: the reference image in, 5 s of the character doing it out, with soundmotion_video.py
  3. Read the posethe figure cut out of each of the 124 frames; ViTPose reads 17 points - 12 joints, nose, eyes, earsvideo_motion.py
  4. Nearest capturethe closest of 2,453 Mixamo clips, with camera angle, mirror and timing (dynamic time warping)match
  5. Fit it to the videoeach bone turned to follow the video on the video's own body - its image from the video, its depth from its length; the head turned to the face--fit
  6. Clip onto the rigretargeted, then the character's own limbs, spine and head aimed along the fit, feet plantedretarget.py
  7. Refine against the videothe character's mesh re-posed frame by frame until its silhouette, joints and face lie on the videorefine_pose.py

How close to the video · the same pose model on both, every frame

Making the rig move the way the video does

The measure: the character's clip is rendered at the video's timing from the video's camera angle, and one pose model reads both. Joints - their mean distance from the video's, in torso lengths. Silhouette - how much of the character's outline overlaps the video's figure (IoU, 1 is identical). Face - the nose, eyes and ears about their own centre: which way the head turns. At rest the 3D model already lies on its reference picture at 0.90-0.93 IoU, so nearly all of what was missing was in the motion.

Where the error came from

The same score at each stage of the old pipeline (joints, torso lengths). Fitting the capture to the video and writing it as an FBX lost nothing; moving it onto the character's own rig doubled or tripled the error.

What fixed it

  1. Fit on the video's own bodyeach bone as long as the video shows it at its longest. On the Mixamo actor's lengths, Aoi's short torso - her legs 1.03 torso lengths to Mixamo's 0.80 - came out as a 28° lean back. Fit error 0.061 → 0.035.video_motion.py
  2. Aim the character's own limbscopying rotations carried the two skeletons' rest-pose differences into every frame - thighs 4-12°, collarbones 16-27°. Aimed along the fit, the rig lands within 0.5° of it.retarget.py
  3. Turn the head to the facethe capture's head looked down and aside; the video's nose, eyes and ears, held against where the pose model finds them on the character's own head, cut face error 2-3 times.calibrate_joints.py
  4. Refine against the videothe character's skinned mesh, posed in PyTorch and seen by the video's camera, has 20 bones nudged each frame until its silhouette, joints and face lie on the video: +0.07-0.10 IoU on its own.refine_pose.py
  5. Put Pip's elbows backrigged 63% of the way down the arm - measured along a centre line that wandered through his puffy sleeve. Now 55%, joint to joint, where the video shows him bend.refine_joints.py

Before and now

What is left

The pose model's blind spots. At the top of the kick it loses the leg either side of the apex - ankle confidence 0.24-0.40, the points bunched at the hip - and the rig follows it: frames 56-70 overlap the video at IoU 0.54, the rest of the kick at 0.86. A correction a few degrees at a time cannot swing a leg 90°. And its 17 points stop at the wrists, so hands keep the capture's angle.

Parts fused to the body. Aoi's hair and Pip's vest are one surface with what they lie on, so they cannot move apart from it (tried for the vest today - see below).

Tried: DWPose, a whole-body model (133 points - body, feet, face and 21 per hand; 134 MB, Apache-2.0). It does not rescue the kick: at the apex it folds the leg too, the ankle at her fists at confidence 0.16-0.21, where ViTPose's three good frames (62-64) follow it to the foot at 0.58-0.68. It does read hands, at median confidence 0.75-0.88 - and the rig's hands now follow them (Hands).

Hands · DWPose's 21 points a hand, on the video and on the render

Turning the hands to the video

ViTPose's 17 points stop at the wrists, so every clip kept the motion capture's hands: Aoi's palms faced down where the video's face the camera, and Pip's hands hung half open where the video makes fists. DWPose reads each hand as 21 points - the wrist, three joints a finger and the tips - and the refine stage now turns the wrists, the forearms about their length and the thirty finger joints until the character's hands lie on that reading. Each finger joint bends only as a finger does - toward the palm, and apart at the knuckles - within a finger's range. The same model reads the render to score it.

The hands, scored

DWPose on the video and on the render, every hand it is sure of: the points about the wrist in palm lengths (lower is better), how often the palm faces the video's way, how far off the hand points. Body scores held: joints, silhouette and face within 0.006 of before on all four moves.

The fit, measured · library clips as ground truth

Bending the nearest capture toward the video

The test: take a library clip, show it to the matcher from a random camera angle with pose-model noise, a 3 s stretch and a speed change, and measure the recovered 3D pose against the true one, in torso lengths. Run once with the clip in the library, and once with it and its near-twins taken out - a move the library does not have. The way the body faces is not counted: a game's controller turns it.

Running it on the Mac · MiniMax H3 is a 33B-parameter model

What it took to run H3 on 24 GB

H3 is built for data-centre GPUs. The fine-tune ships in ComfyUI's 4-bit format; three problems stood between it and this laptop.

Apple's GPU has no float8
11.8 → 15.4 GB, lossless

The fine-tune's per-group scales are float8, which PyTorch on Apple GPUs cannot hold - the first layer failed. They are rewritten as float32 once (f8_scales_to_f32.py, 21 s); a real layer then matched the original on the CPU to 0.036%.

ComfyUI's LoRA patching swapped the Mac to a halt
20 min stuck → loads in 1 min

On a Mac ComfyUI loads a model whole and keeps a backup of every weight a LoRA patches: 15 GB became 30. It also requantizes each weight - 4.5% error to carry the turbo LoRA's 0.02% change. Baked in offline instead, on each layer's own 4-bit grid: 0.1% of codes moved, 0.5% noise, 7.5 min on the CPU (the GPU took 53 s a layer to the CPU's 1.1).

Time per clip
32–48 min / 5 s

Text encoder (Qwen3-VL 32B) on the CPU: 87–108 s. Video model load: ~1 min. Four sampling steps: 29 min at 6–7.5 min each - attention over ~13,000 video tokens is the cost. Decoding: ~4 min. Everything else on the Mac slows down while it runs.

A bug the preview caught
elbows 173° → 54°

The first clip on Mara's rig had its arms stuck out in a T-pose: Blender had exported the boxing guard as the skeleton's rest, so every guard read as "no change from rest". The FBX now exports with the capture's T-pose showing, and the round trip checks it.

Open: Pip's vest lifts into wings

A move from video found what the 18 standard clips never reach: with his arms overhead, Pip's puffy vest rises with his collarbones. The vest and his hoodie sleeve are one fused surface, both labelled clothing. Told apart by colour, the vest is found exactly - but tied to the chest it tears where it meets the sleeve, and faded in from its edge the wings stay. It needs to be its own layer, generated or cut that way.

Also open

Poses the pose model rarely saw (the top of a high kick - DWPose misses it too); hands DWPose cannot be sure of - blurred by a fast move, under hair, overhead at the frame's edge (Pip's); joints that leave the frame; H3's licence for anything public; a licence-clean video model (Wan 2.2, Apache-2.0) as the alternative.
