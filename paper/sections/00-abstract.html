Abstract

We describe CharForge, a pipeline that turns a few words or a single picture into a game-ready character - a textured mesh with a standard skeleton, modelled hands, a face rig and eighteen animation clips, exported as glTF and FBX - running end to end on one Apple Silicon laptop with local models only. A second lane adds new moves from generated video: a reference-to-video model films the character performing a written move, a pose model reads the video, the nearest motion capture is retrieved and bent toward it, and the character's own skinned mesh is optimised until it lies on the video. Across four moves in three art styles the rig's silhouette overlaps the video's figure at IoU {{moves.iou.min}}-{{moves.iou.max}} (from {{moves.iou0.min}}-{{moves.iou0.max}}), with joints {{moves.joints.min}}-{{moves.joints.max}} torso lengths from the video's. A third lane makes a move from a sentence, generated on the character's own skeleton in about half a minute. Every frame of every clip is audited for aberrations - faces crushed, stretched or sheared, one body part inside another, a shoe through the floor - and holding each clip inside joint limits measured on the character's own mesh takes the frames with a part more than 2 cm inside another from {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}% to {{aberr.same.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}%. Measured against professionally made meshes, the models' surfaces were two to four times rougher; generating at a million faces with the surface filtered, keeping the pose the model was made in as its rest pose, and mending what close-ups of every character showed - loose shards, two-toned hands, one skin in three colours - brought the roster's median surface noise from {{q.summary.noise_deg_before|2}}° to {{q.summary.noise_deg_after|2}}° and its loose pieces from {{q.summary.pieces_before}} to {{q.summary.pieces_after}}. Faces, the largest gap left, were then taken part by part - each eye rebuilt as a smooth cap under its lids, each feature laid on the model's own, the face left as the picture painted it - which on a benchmark set of two characters per style and gender took the median likeness of the eyes to the picture from {{f.summary.like_eyes_before|2}} to {{f.summary.like_eyes_after_paired|2}} and of the mouth from {{f.summary.like_mouth_before|2}} to {{f.summary.like_mouth_after_paired|2}} - on realistic faces most; drawn faces held. The work is organised as a record of measured decisions: {{n.experiments_done}} experiments, each with its question, metric and result, and what was dropped kept beside what was kept. Several of the largest errors we found were in our own measurements - a comparison video running 25% fast, a score dip that was the pose model misreading our render, face positions read in 3.9 mm steps - and we report how each was caught. This is a living paper: its tables are regenerated from the pipeline's measurement files, and its roadmap ({{n.experiments_planned}} planned or open experiments) is the project's.
