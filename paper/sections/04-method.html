Method

We describe each stage by what it does and the failure that made it necessary. Experiment identifiers link to the tracker, where each has its question, metric and result.

A few words, written out

Users type little: "a boy scout", "gray alien that is extremely muscular". Passed straight to an image model, the first came back a grown man at the height slider's default of 1.72 m and the second a grey clay sculpture - no clothes, no colour. Following Make-A-Character's use of a language model to parse a prompt into attributes [@mach], a local model [@gemma] fills a fixed set of fields - who, age, build, skin, hair, face, top, bottom, shoes, extras - and the sentence is composed from them in code. Asked instead for free prose under rules, 3B-8B models broke a rule in about one prompt in two ("holding a wooden badge", "standing at 1.5 meters tall"); fields that name a prop, a pose or a height are now dropped, and the age group is taken from the prompt's own words, not the model's. The height follows the age group unless the user sets it, and a name that exists is refused rather than overwritten [E016].

We scored nine local models on five prompts against four rules each [E015]; [#tab-prompts] gives the results. Gemma 2 9B kept all 20 checks but took {{pm.gemma2:9b-instruct-q5_K_M.secs|1}} s a prompt; Gemma 4 e4b kept {{pm.gemma4:e4b.score}} in {{pm.gemma4:e4b.secs|1}} s and is the default. Gemma 4 answered nothing until its "thinking" mode was switched off, which only models that report the capability are sent. The Studio shows the written-out text before anything is drawn, to edit.

The reference image

Everything is generated from one picture of the character standing in an A-pose on a plain background. We compared two local image models on six characters with the same prompt and seed [E013]: Krea 2 drew one broken figure in six (a jacket with no head or legs, which the automatic checks passed, since it still has "arms"), Qwen-Image 2.1 [@qwenimage21] produced no broken figures and returned a usable alpha cut-out in each case. Qwen read "arms slightly away from the body" literally - wrists 0.28-0.37 torso lengths from the hips, close enough to fuse in 3D - until the angle was spelled out (0.58-0.70). It is the default; Krea stays a flag away. Qwen-Image 2.1's license is limited to non-commercial research and evaluation; commercial use requires a separate license [@qwenimage21license].

A reference in the wrong pose costs half an hour before it shows. Bo, a cartoon chef, was drawn with his fists on his hips despite the prompt; the 3D model fused them and his hands came out in shreds at his belt. The pose model now reads every reference's arms: our references put the wrists 0.60-0.79 torso lengths from the hips, Bo's 0.33, and below 0.45 the picture is drawn again on a new seed [E028].

The 3D model, checked from the front

TRELLIS.2 [@trellis2] builds a mesh with PBR textures from the reference. On four characters its first pass built the picture's plain backdrop into the model as a solid board - in front of the figure, behind it, or through its legs - which later stages cut into grey sheets hanging from the arms ([#fig-board]). Rendered from the front, a sound model lies on the picture's silhouette at IoU {{b.boards.sound.0}}-{{b.boards.sound.1}}; these lay at 0.35-0.44. The model is now checked from the front, and below 0.8 made again from the picture cut out by a salient-object model [@u2net], then on a new seed [E014]: Gray {{b.boards.gray.0}} → {{gray.front_iou}}, Knight {{b.boards.knight.0}} → {{knight.front_iou}}, Kaito {{b.boards.kaito.0}} → {{kaito.front_iou}}, the boy scout {{b.boards.boyscout.0}} → {{boyscout.front_iou}}.

A second pass conditions TRELLIS on side and back views repainted from renders of the first. Scored against a generated 360° video of Mara [E012], the second pass moved the mean silhouette IoU from {{b.turntable.reference_only}} to {{b.turntable.repainted}}, and three turntable frames as extra views to {{b.turntable.turntable_frames}}: more views barely change the shape, and where views disagree the model averages them (Mara's braid, over her shoulder in the reference and down her back in the video, was lost at the back). The second pass is also the slowest stage ([#tab-times]). Every character built with it keeps both models, so the two were held against each other on {{mv.n}} characters [E106] ([#fig-e106]). The second model fits the reference's front worse on {{mv.worse_n}} of {{mv.n}} (silhouette IoU {{mv.front_pass1}} on average for the first pass, {{mv.front_mesh}} for the second), and its side silhouettes overlap the first's by only {{mv.side_cross_min}}-{{mv.side_cross_max}}: it is a different model, not a refinement. Where the difference shows it follows the repaint, not the picture. The image model repainting the side and back views invents what the reference does not show, and the second pass builds it: the boy scout grew knee socks and lost his grin, Vex's trainers became boots and a grey hood appeared under her jacket, Mara's braid moved from her shoulder to her back. What it adds is a more plausible back (the scout's neckerchief is tied behind him). The second pass is therefore off by default since this version (--quality best keeps it), which saves a median {{stage.multiview.min}} of {{stage.total.min}} minutes. This is a pilot on finished models: the end-to-end comparison, with the characters rigged and audited both ways and rated blind, is still to run.

A finer surface. TRELLIS.2 simplifies its surface before writing it. At the 200,000 faces this pipeline asked for until version 0.11, gently curved armour became large flat facets with sawtooth edges, and every later stage kept them: the solid's vertices are put back on the generated surface to keep its detail, so the facets reached the normal map and showed as a triangle pattern under any grazing light ([#fig-rake]). The generator now keeps 1M faces, and its surface is filtered before anything is built on it by bilateral normal filtering [@bnf]: each face normal is averaged with its neighbours', weighted by their area, their distance and how alike the two normals are, so facets a few degrees apart become one smooth surface while a plate's edge, tens of degrees sharp, stays; the vertices are then moved to agree with the filtered normals [@sunnormal], a median 0.05 mm on a 1M-face model [E135]. A rebuild of the roster on it is scored in [#sec-eval-quality]. Quad remeshing was tried as well and refused both the generated mesh and the solid (QuadriFlow needs a manifold input).

Solid, joints, arms and hands

The generated mesh is closed into one solid (OpenVDB level sets [@openvdb]), its flakes, inner shells and shards removed. Orbit renders give per-vertex part labels from a SegFormer architecture [@segformer] using the FASHN human-parsing checkpoint [@fashnparser] and 3D joints from the pose model; each joint is then moved onto the centre line of its limb, traced through the solid. A traced line can double back: on knight2 the arm's trace reached his gauntlet and turned 36 cm back up the vambrace, and the hand was placed a forearm's length behind the wrist; the trace now ends at its farthest point [E018].

Arms cut free. Generators glue arms to whatever they hang against - a hip, a vest, a bag. Rigged as is, the surface between moves with both, and a raised arm pulls the vest into wings. The arms are cut free below the armpit (T-pose faces stretched past 3x: 1,150 → 390 on Pip), thin webs opened first, and the cut checked slab by slab along the arm and repeated where a join survives: 3-9 joined slabs an arm after the first cut, 1-3 after the second [E025] [E026].

Modelled hands. Generated hands are paddles. They are carved off at the wrist and replaced by a modelled hand (a signed distance function with anatomical proportions, 15 finger bones) unioned onto the solid. The modelled wrist was a person's; on Gray, "extremely muscular", whose forearms measure 11-13 cm at the wrist, each arm ended in a flat step like a cuff and the waving hand read as a small hook ([#fig-hands-bulk]). A sleeve's cuff measures just as wide, so width cannot tell a bulky forearm from a sleeve - but the part labels can: the vertices just above the cut are labelled bare skin on 0.92-1.00 of a bare forearm and 0.13 or less under a sleeve or gauntlet. On a bare forearm the hand is now scaled across its width and thickness to meet it, up to 1.6x (Gray {{gray.hand_bulk|2}}x) [E021].

Mesh and texture

The solid is decimated to about 60,000 triangles [@qem], the head keeping {{f.summary.head_share_median|int}}% of them, unwrapped, and its colour, normal, roughness/metal and ambient occlusion baked from the generated model. Collapse decimation spends triangles where the surface turns and left the head 17% of the budget, too few for an eyelid or a lip; a vertex group lets the head's vertices collapse less freely, its strength found by bisection until the share holds [E140]. The generator's own colour is a 1024-px atlas of thousands of small charts for a million faces - most triangles smaller than a texel - and along the charts' edges it leaves texels sampled off the surface; baked, they were a crackle of short grey and dark lines over every surface the picture does not paint ([#fig-crackle]). They are not dark specks a filter on the atlas can find, nor holes in the mesh; and an atlas texel cannot be mended from its neighbours there, which belong to other charts. So the colour is rebuilt through the mesh: each triangle sampled at its centroid, each vertex (its copies at the atlas's seams joined) given the median of its triangles' colours, a vertex far from its neighbours' median along the mesh given that median, and the atlas painted again from the vertex colours at four times its resolution, where nearly every triangle holds a texel of its own. New characters are generated at 2048 px. Smart UV projection on a smoothed copy folded islands onto themselves wherever the copy curls ({{b.uv_folds.before.0}}-{{b.uv_folds.before.1}} faces per character sharing texels with another surface - the backs of Pip's sleeves showed his vest's paint); the overlapping faces are unwrapped again as their own islands at one texel density, leaving {{b.uv_folds.after.0}}-{{b.uv_folds.after.1}} [E027].

The bake is soft, so the reference is projected back onto the mesh, aligned to renders of the mesh by a similarity transform on the silhouettes and then by a smooth optical flow [@dis], together with the repainted side and back views; the reference sets the palette, colour-matched group by group. Like CharacterGen's back-projection [@charactergen] a view only paints surfaces it sees face-on and unoccluded; unlike it, views are blended by weight rather than by Poisson blending [@poisson] - comparing the two is [E105]. The face is painted from a detailed image: the reference's face region enlarged 3-5x and re-detailed by the image model at low strength.

The face in parts. Faces looked sloppy after version 0.12 - eyes hollow or smeared, features off the shape, patches on the cheek - and taken one part at a time the faults had separate causes [E140]. The eyes were in the shape: TRELLIS.2 models every eye as an almond opening with shards inside it, the solid filled it with streaks, and the decimation crumpled it into a pit no texture reads as an eye ([#fig-eyes]). Each opening is found by DWPose [@dwpose] on a front render of the generated model in its own colours - the generator paints its features where its geometry has them, where on a grey render of a drawn face the pose model only guessed - and rebuilt in the solid as a cap across the opening: it follows the lids' depth round the rim, bulges a little forward in the middle like an eyeball under lids and sinks a little at the rim; what lies in front of it is cleared, the region smoothed and kept from being put back onto the generated surface's shards. A ball set behind the opening instead left a slot whose hard ledges rendered as dark rings round the eyes. The features were in the projection: the silhouette fit had put Mara's painted face larger than her sculpted one, its eyes 10-30 pixels off. Each part - each brow, each eye, the nose, the mouth, the jaw - is now moved by its own similarity [@umeyama] from where the projection puts the model's points (read on the generator's paint) to the picture's, and the moves are spread by a thin-plate spline [@tps] that falls to nothing past the face; this replaces the earlier face flow, which had squinted and smeared Mara's eyes. The face's ellipse is painted by the front view alone - the generated side views, their faces drawn again a little elsewhere, put patches on the cheek and skin on the hair - and left out of the skin tone below ([#fig-faceparts]). Anime faces are not laid on in parts: a drawn face's shape does not say how big its eyes are, only the generator's paint does, and fitted to it Kaito's eyes came out a fifth wider than his picture's. Where DWPose cannot read the model's eyes ({{f.summary.n}} benchmark characters: {{f.summary.eyes_rebuilt_chars}} rebuilt; most anime faces are not read) the eyes stay as generated, and where it cannot read both faces the face flow is used as before. A drawn smile can run past the lips - Cadet's, Kaito's and Bo's corners read 1.2 eye spacings apart - so the jaw's cut is held to 0.8 of the eye spacing.

No loose shards. The solid is one piece, but the modelled hands are unioned onto it and the decimation's repairs delete faces round non-manifold edges, so small shells survived into every low-poly: 2-94 a character, of 1-58 faces each and a few millimetres to 3 cm across, floating at Cadet's and Knight2's shoulders, and holding nearly all of the model's open edges (Cadet 207 of 212). Pieces under 0.25% of the faces are dropped; a garment or a strand kept apart by design is hundreds of faces or more [E138].

Hands shaded by their own shape. The modelled hands take the skin tone of the face, and took their light and dark from the base colour bake - which comes from the generated mesh, where the old hand, or nothing, stands where the modelled hand is - so every hand carried pale and dark patches. They now take it from the ambient occlusion baked on the solid they are part of: soft shade in the creases between the fingers, nothing elsewhere ([#fig-hands-ao]) [E138].

One skin. The skin's colour comes from three places - the picture, with its light and shadow, on the front; the generator's colours on the sides and back; one tone on the modelled hands - so the same skin was three colours: a ring at every bare wrist (Mara's forearm L* 50 against her hand's 69, CIEDE2000 16.6) and, on the face, the picture's shading lying on a shape not quite the picture's. Skin texels - near the character's skin tone in hue and chroma, at a lightness within a band of it so that shading counts and brows, lashes, lips and eyes do not, on the parts the parser calls skin (brown boots and khaki are skin-coloured too) - have their colour at the 1-2 cm scale of shading replaced by the tone, measured in 3D so a cheek's islands are not kept apart by the atlas, and keep their own detail at the scale of freckles and pores. Mara's wrists go from dE 16.6 and 16.3 to 1.5 and 2.2 [E138]. Anime is left as drawn: its painted shading is the style. So is the face, since version 0.13: its colour bands cut the picture's soft cheek shadow into patches, some texels lifted and their neighbours not, and the face is now the picture's own [E140].

Colours a renderer can light. A physically based renderer multiplies the light by the base colour, and the PBR guides keep a non-metal between sRGB 30-50 and 240 [@pbrguide]: a black jacket painted at sRGB 5-15 renders as a hole without folds, and 17-38% of five characters' textures lay below 30. Every non-metal texel's lightness is eased into that range by a smooth curve that leaves texels well inside it untouched and keeps a garment's own contrast, its hue and chroma as painted; metals (the ORM map's metal channel) are left alone [E132].

Painted sides and backs, on request. Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1 [@hunyuan3d21], run through its Apple Silicon port [@hunyuanmac], paints six views of the mesh from its own normals and positions, consistent with the picture and each other. Its own bake back onto the atlas covered a third of the surface on our meshes and filled the rest in UV space, a patchwork, so only its views are kept, fitted onto our cameras by silhouette and projected like any view, with the front delit by Hunyuan's delighting model and each garment's colour on the side and back views matched to the front's [E133] [E134]. It cleaned Pip's blotchy back. On eight more characters, though, the texture audit scored it better while it looked worse on six: generated at 256 px, the views paint what the picture does not show - a face-like pattern on the back of the boy scout's head - and lose fine pattern ([#fig-views]). It is an option (--texture-views hunyuan), used for Pip.

Weights and rig

Skin weights come from distances measured through the solid body rather than through the air [@geodesicvoxel], so a vest that touches an arm is still far from the arm's bone. Three rules handle garments the generator fused to the body [E025]. The arm's own surface is flooded from the sleeve's outside starting past the cut (0.30 of the upper arm), so it does not walk onto the vest; a vest's armhole moves with the collarbone where colour tells the torso garment from the sleeve; and an upper garment near the hips follows the pelvis rather than the thighs, so a jump does not pull a hem into a W. Loose pieces the generator left (Kaito came as 40: hair spikes, the inner toes of his sandals) each ride rigidly with the body part nearest them. The project rig maps the 64-bone Mixamo-style vocabulary in its clip library, including fingers and a jaw [@mixamomirror]; bone chains are hung on what should swing (braids, ponytails, bags). The glTF standard's skinning path is linear blend [@gltf]; the playground patches three.js [@threejs] to dual quaternion skinning [@dqs], which cuts jacket faces collapsed below half their area during the walk from {{b.dqs.walk.0}}% to {{b.dqs.walk.1}}% and during the run from {{b.dqs.run.0}}% to {{b.dqs.run.1}}% with the same weights [E024].

The pose it was modelled in, as its rest. Retargeting turns each source bone by its change from its own rest and applies that change to the target's rest, so the two rests must agree: Mixamo's is a T. Until version 0.11 the rig was therefore skinned up from the A-pose the character was generated in to a T and saved in it - and skinning is linear blending, which cannot be undone exactly: armour fused to the arm (Cadet's pauldrons) sheared into shards on the way up, and arms-down frames, skinned back down from that T, kept slivers. The T is now recorded on the armature and used only inside the retargeting maths; the rest pose stays the modelled one. The clips come out the same - foot slip identical in all nine gaits - while on Cadet crushed faces went from {{q.e137_cadet.T.crushed_pct}}% to {{q.e137_cadet.A.crushed_pct}}%, self-intersecting face pairs per frame from {{q.e137_cadet.T.intersecting_pairs_mean|int}} to {{q.e137_cadet.A.intersecting_pairs_mean|int}}, and frames with a limb more than 2 cm inside another from {{q.e137_cadet.T.deep_frames_pct|0}}% to {{q.e137_cadet.A.deep_frames_pct|0}}% ([#fig-rest]) [E137]. Arms raised above the shoulder still stretch armour fused to the arm; that needs rigid shoulder pieces [E110]. Engines take an A-pose bind like any other (Unity's Enforce T-Pose, Unreal's retarget pose, Godot's Fix Silhouette); --rest T bakes the T as before, and the manifest says which a build has.

Face rig

The head is rendered from the front, unlit, together with the world position under every pixel. Eyes are found as blobs and the mouth by its colour and the pose model's face points; from them the face stage cuts the mouth open, adds a jaw bone and five shapes (blinks, smile, raised brows, pucker). It places nothing it cannot find: on Bo, eyes behind round glasses and a mouth under a moustache, it builds no face rig rather than a guessed one [E109]. Two characters lost their face rig because the frame stopped above the chin on a tall head [E017]. The position render had its own error: written as position + 4 into EEVEE's half-float film, every position was read in {{b.face_quantisation.before_mm}} mm steps, {{b.face_quantisation.offset_mean_mm}} mm off on average; written around the frame's centre the step is {{b.face_quantisation.after_mm}} mm [E019].

Clips, feet and packaging

Eighteen motion captures (idle, walk, jog, run, sprint, backpedals, strafes, turns, crouch, jump, fall, land, wave) are retargeted onto the rig with the fingers. Feet are planted by clip selection, grounding and foot IK, and the playground drives all locomotion from one stride clock, so the foot on the floor slides a median {{feet.median}}% of the gait's ground speed ([#sec-eval-feet]) [E029]. The foot that is planted is the shoe as the viewer deforms it: every vertex of the shoe below the ankle ({{pl.points_per_foot.0}}-{{pl.points_per_foot.1}} a foot on the roster), each posed by its own skin weights with dual quaternions, the way the playground skins the mesh [E130]. A planted key pivots on the flat of the sole; a key in the air keeps the whole shoe, toe cap included, above the floor. A rigid copy carried on the ankle bone works for an ordinary shoe, which is rigid below the ankle; Cadet's armoured boots blend with the shin above a high ankle joint, and the rigid copy stood on the floor while the boot on screen was 4.4 cm under it. All of the shoe is needed, not a sample of it: thinned to 400 points, its lowest point jumped from one sample to the next as the foot rolled, and the planted pivot jumped with it. The package follows engine conventions: real height in metres, origin on the floor under the pelvis, glTF [@gltf] and FBX with three levels of detail, and a manifest with the collision capsule, each clip's ground speed, direction and contact phase, the jump's take-off and landing, and the face's morph targets.

Room for each limb

A motion capture was performed by one person of one build. Played on a thicker character, the same joint angles fold a forearm into a puffy sleeve, a heel into a thigh, and hang the arms inside a wide torso. Game rigs carry joint limits for this; we measure them for each character from its own mesh [E128]. From the rest pose each elbow and knee is bent in 5° steps about its hinge axis, read from the character's own clips as the mean axis of each frame's bend, and the upper arm is lowered from the T-pose at five swings, from 60° forward to 60° back. At every step the mesh is posed and the depth of one body region inside another measured as in [#sec-eval-aberr]. A joint's limit is the last angle before that depth passes 2 cm, the audit's own threshold for a deep penetration: flesh squashes where a rigid mesh can only overlap, and at a 1 cm tolerance Mara's boxing guard was opened by 25°. The arms' limits leave out the head, whose region includes the hair; hair should swing aside, not stop an arm.

Every clip is then held inside the limits. An arm past its limit moves to the nearest allowed direction, measured against the chest so a lean does not count, and those turns are smoothed over ±2 keys so the nearest choice cannot hop between two near-equal ones (on Bo's first limits, raising the arm instead cost up to 44° where moving it out to the side cost 17°), and faded out as the arm swings past 60° forward or back, where the limits were not measured. An elbow or knee past its limit is opened about its own bend axis, a knee only while its foot is off the floor (faded in between 8 and 16 cm of lift), so the feet stay where retargeting planted them. The limits are soft: the last 10° are approached along a tanh, so a correction has no corner in time. The sweeps take about a minute per character and the clearance seconds; both run at the end of the animate stage. Contact-aware retargeting treats the same interpenetration with a learned, geometry-conditioned model [@contactretarget]; measured limits need no training data and cannot invent a pose, but they only see each joint alone (see [#sec-eval-aberr] for what that leaves).

Moves from generated video

The video. The character's reference image and a written move - timed beats: ready, the move, recovery - go to the community Ref2VA fine-tune [@h3] based on MiniMax H3 [@h3base], which returns 5.2 s at 24 fps of the character performing it (32-48 minutes on the laptop, [E030]); its text encoder and VAEs are supplied by a separate Comfy-Org package [@h3comfy]. Because the performer is the character, its proportions are close to the rig's.

Retrieval. ViTPose [@vitpose] reads 17 points in every frame. The sequence is matched against a library of 2,453 captures from the pinned mirror [@mixamomirror] by dynamic time warping [@dtw] of each clip's 2D projection from 16 camera angles, mirrored or not. In a self-test on 40 library clips shown from random angles with pose noise, a stretch and a speed change, the right clip or an exact duplicate ranked first every time, and the angle was off by {{st.left_out.angle_error_deg_median}}° (median) [E001].

The fit. A move the library lacks is approximated by its nearest capture, then bent toward the video bone by bone: each bone's direction in the image from the video, its depth from its length - kept from the capture where the two agree within the pose model's noise, and only when the capture misses the video by more than 1.2 times that noise [E002]. The bone lengths are the video's own, measured where the capture shows each bone flat to the camera [E003]: with a capture actor's lengths, Aoi's short torso bent into a 28° lean. [#tab-selftest] shows the trade-off on the self-test: on the actor's own body the capture's lengths are, as they must be, slightly better; on another body - the real case - the video's own lengths are clearly better. An arm's two depth signs are chosen together, so an elbow cannot bend past what the library's elbows ever do [E007].

Onto the character, and refined. The fitted clip is retargeted by copying rotations and then aiming the character's own limbs, spine and head along the fitted bones, which removes rest-pose differences of 4-12° at the thighs and 16-27° at the collarbones [E004]. Finally the character's own skinned mesh is posed in PyTorch - forward kinematics mirroring Blender's - projected with the video's camera, and 20 bones a frame are optimised against the video in the manner of SMPLify [@smplify], but on the character rather than a body model [E005]: the silhouette's chamfer both ways, the joints, the face points, DWPose's hands [@dwpose] (wrists, forearm twist and 30 finger joints [E006]), a prior to the fitted clip, smoothness, joint limits, and a bend limit at the elbow. The feet are then put back on the video's floor line [E008].

Moves from words

The capture library has no salute, no boxing guard, no march. A video of the character doing the move works ([#sec-m-motion]) but takes half an hour of video generation per move. UniMate [@unimate] generates a skeletal motion from a sentence for a skeleton it has never seen: a flow-matching model conditioned on the skeleton's topology and T-pose as well as on the text, encoded by Flan-T5 [@flant5]. We run it on each character's own skeleton [E129]. UniMate's own preprocessing reads the packaged character - its canonical T-pose, its topology and its clips as motion features - through Blender 4.0 as a Python module, the version it was written for. Its sampler integrates the flow with an adaptive solver that needs double precision, which Apple's GPU lacks; a fixed 50-step Euler grid runs on the GPU in about 25 s per two-second move. UniMate's exporter writes an FBX on the character's canonical rig, and the move then goes through retargeting, foot planting and clearance ([#sec-m-clearance]) like any captured clip. Two of our conventions broke it. Face-rig morph targets kept their old coordinates when UniMate moved the mesh into its canonical frame, so the export tore into ribbons even for the character's own clips; UniMate gets a copy without them, since it only makes bone motion. And its pruned skeleton ends at the toe bone, which the FBX importer drops as a leaf, so the generated moves went unplanted; the foot bone's tail now stands in for a missing toe.

Each move is sampled three times and the best kept by eye ([#fig-unimate]; automating the choice is [E143]). Prompts follow the wording of UniMate's training captions ("An object marches in place."): a longer, more specific sentence for the march and for looking around produced lunges and hands on the head, the short one a march.

Running it on a laptop

Everything runs on a 24 GB M5 MacBook with shared memory. Two jobs on the GPU at once do not merely slow down: two refines corrupted each other's results (Aoi's silhouette {{b.gpu_overlap.aoi_iou.0}} → {{b.gpu_overlap.aoi_iou.1}}, the kick's {{b.gpu_overlap.kick_iou.0}} → {{b.gpu_overlap.kick_iou.1}}, where each alone reaches {{b.gpu_overlap.alone.0}}-{{b.gpu_overlap.alone.1}}; one ended in NaN), and a 3D pass beside another job was stopped by the operating system's GPU watchdog. A machine-wide lock now serialises every GPU step of every job [E023]. The video model is a 33-billion-parameter model; running it here took rewriting its float8 scales for a GPU without float8 and merging its LoRA offline, since patching it at load doubled its memory [E030]. The Studio ([#fig-studio-new]) queues jobs, survives its own restart mid-job, and shows each job's stage and time left.
