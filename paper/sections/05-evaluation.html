Evaluation

Measures

We measure what a player would see, in units that do not depend on the character's size.

  • Joint error (moves from video): the character's clip is rendered at the video's own frame times from the video's camera angle; the same pose model [@vitpose] reads the video and the render, the hips are laid on each other frame by frame with one scale for the clip, and the mean distance between the twelve body joints is reported in the video's torso lengths (shoulders to hips; 0.05 is about 2.5 cm on an adult).
  • Silhouette IoU: the render's outline against the video's figure, cut out by a salient-object model [@u2net].
  • Face error: nose, eyes and ears about their centre, in torso lengths - which way the head turns.
  • Hands: DWPose [@dwpose] on both, each hand it is sure of; the 21 points about the wrist in palm lengths (mean and median), how often the palm faces the video's way, and the angle between the hands' pointing directions.
  • Per stage: the same joint error for the fitted points, the FBX written from them, the character's own skeleton, and the pose model's reading of the render - which separates the rig's error from the scorer's.
  • Four views: the clip rendered orthographically from the video's camera, the side and above, at one scale on a 10 cm grid, with each elbow's and knee's bend and each shoe's height above the floor read out per frame.
  • Foot slip: while a foot is on the floor, how far its lowest vertex slides between frames, as a share of the gait's actual nonzero ground speed, driven by the playground's own controller; absolute slip in m/s is also retained. A percentage is undefined for a stationary gait. The historical table used a 1 m/s denominator floor and is provisional below that speed; Blender's corrected v2 path and the still-legacy browser audit also need contact-mask parity before the same-schedule rerun [E029] [E122]. Sole depth remains a separate fixed-floor measure, not contact-conditioned penetration. Human perception thresholds from mannequin studies are rating-design evidence, not a universal pass value [@footskateperception].
  • Front silhouette IoU of a 3D model against its reference picture, rendered from the front.
  • Aberrations [E102]: every frame of every clip of every character, on the mesh as the viewers deform it (dual quaternion skinning), against its rest pose. Faces crushed below half their rest area or stretched past twice it; faces sheared, turned more than 60° beyond their own bone; triangles that intersect although they were more than 3 cm apart at rest [@blenderbvhtree]; penetration, how deep one body region has gone inside another, counted as deep past 2 cm; vertices under the floor; and popping, a vertex whose offset from its neighbours spikes out and back within three frames. Each is assigned to the body region of the bone that dominates the surface there. Shares are of all faces, so characters compare directly.

Seeing every move from four sides

One camera cannot see depth. A clip can match its video from the video's angle while an arm folds back through itself behind the figure. We render every move from the video's camera, the side and above at once, beside the video, with the joints read out ([#fig-angles]) [E009]. It found three faults the scores had missed: Aoi's elbow bent to {{b.elbow.before_deg}}° (the forearm back through the upper arm) for {{b.elbow.frames_before}} frames, now held at {{b.elbow.after_deg}}° [E007]; the feet of every move standing off the floor after the refine, Aoi's lowest shoe {{b.feet_refine.before_mean_cm}} cm up on average, now {{b.feet_refine.after_mean_cm}} cm [E008]; and a comparison video running 25% fast [E010]. It also cleared the rig of a fault the score seemed to show [E011].

Feet

The archived E029 table below reports the historical post-change foot-slip audit. A review found that its normalization divides by max(manifest speed, 1 m/s): values at or above 1 m/s are normalized to the stated speed, values below 1 m/s are understated, and stationary percentages are undefined. The historical measurements are preserved, but the cross-gait aggregate is provisional until E122 recomputes absolute slip speed and the true-speed ratio at the same 60 fps schedule. The current browser helper also retains that denominator floor; its contact-transition rule differs from the Blender v2 path, so the two implementations must be reconciled or reported as different estimands. The earlier pre-change range is not directly comparable until both sides are rerun under the corrected metric. The worst visual cases previously noted were strafes and crouch-walks on characters whose proportions differ most from the capture actor's.

Aberrations

The aberration audit was checked before it was believed. A synthetic character (a box torso and an arm on two bones with a blended elbow) was built with aberrations of known size, and {{controls.passed}} of {{controls.n}} checks pass under both skinnings ([#tab-controls]): nothing where nothing moves; exactly the share of faces built to be crushed or stretched; the floor at −3 cm; a one-frame pop found on its own frame and a fast smooth bend not called one; and penetrations of 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 cm measured to the millimetre. Three of these checks failed first, and each failure was an error that would have misled the measurements of real characters ([#sec-eval-validity]). One-frame pops are only seen on the frames sampled, so the audit samples every frame; that costs a fifth more time than every second one.

On the {{aberr.n}} characters, before clearance, {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.crushed_pct}}% of faces were crushed, {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.stretched_pct}}% stretched and {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.sheared_pct}}% sheared, averaged over clips and characters. Crouch walk, the clip furthest from a standing pose, is among the two worst for every character; the other is the sprint or a strafe. Most crushed faces are out of sight: of the flagged faces, a ray along the normal escapes the character for only {{aberr.min.exposed_crushed_pct|int}}-{{aberr.max.exposed_crushed_pct|int}}% (an armpit collapses under the arm). Dual quaternion skinning, the playground's, crushes fewer faces than linear skinning, the glTF default ({{b.skinning_roster.dqs.crushed_pct}}% against {{b.skinning_roster.lbs.crushed_pct}}%), and stretches more ({{b.skinning_roster.dqs.stretched_pct}}% against {{b.skinning_roster.lbs.stretched_pct}}%): it keeps the volume that linear blending loses, and a joint bulges instead [@dqs]. Penetration was the most common aberration: in {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}% of frames, on average, one body region was more than 2 cm inside another ({{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.deep_frames_pct|int}}% counting arms through hair, which the head's region includes).

Holding the clips inside each character's joint limits ([#sec-m-clearance]) brings that to {{aberr.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}% ({{aberr.mean.deep_frames_pct|int}}% with hair), and the deepest penetration between body regions, averaged over clips, from {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.body_penetration_cm}} to {{aberr.mean.body_penetration_cm}} cm ([#tab-aberrations]). No character's crushed, stretched or sheared share rises, and no pop is added (one hand pop already in Wren's sprint now straddles two frames). The planted feet do not move: Bo's foot slip on walk, jog, run and sprint is identical to the fourth decimal before and after. The largest single turn clearance makes is {{aberr.min.clearance_max_deg|int}}-{{aberr.max.clearance_max_deg|int}}° per character; the armoured Knights, whose plates meet early, take the most.

What clearance leaves is what a single joint's limit cannot see: hands swinging into thighs that are themselves moving (the limits are measured with the legs at rest), legs brushing past each other in strafes, arms through long hair, which is a job for the hair's own spring chains [E125], and knees through a long coat's hem, which needs the hem to follow the thighs.

Validity of the measurements

Several of the largest errors we found were in the measuring, not the pipeline. We list them because each would have sent the work in the wrong direction.

  • The comparison ran fast [E010]. The side-by-side and four-view videos resampled the 24 fps video to the renders' 30 fps by taking each video frame once, never twice, so the video played 25% fast and then stood still: the rig seemed to punch 0.2 s late, more as the clip went on. The fidelity score was unaffected, since it renders the rig at the video's own times.
  • The scorer misread the render [E011]. Mara's kick scored {{kick.joints}}, with a dip to 0.46 at the top of the kick that read as the rig folding its leg. Held against the rig's own skeleton, the pose model's reading of the render was wrong in {{kick.misread_n}} frames (the skeleton was 0.12-0.14 from the video there, as elsewhere); without them the kick scores {{kick.joints_trusted}}. The four views confirm the leg is at head height.
  • Face positions in stairs [E019]: {{b.face_quantisation.before_mm}} mm quantisation from a half-float render target, found while tracing an unrelated seam.
  • Stale caches: the per-stage tool kept its skeleton dumps forever and was scoring a rig two rebuilds old.
  • Triangles that change under the audit [E102]: Blender splits a quad along its shorter diagonal in its current shape, so the synthetic elbow's quads re-split at 55° of bend and a comparison with rest compared different triangles. The audit now splits once, at rest.
  • Depth that stopped at 3 cm: the first penetration search looked a fixed six vertex rings from the intersection and measured a 5 cm push as 3.1 cm. It now floods through every vertex that is still inside.
  • Inside an open tube: a body region is an open patch (the upper arm is a tube open at both ends), and past its open end the nearest face's normal says nothing about inside. Aoi's bent forearm read 15.9 cm inside her upper arm; Vex's elbow read 15.7 cm at 105° of bend and 0 at 115°. A vertex now counts as inside only if it projects straight onto the face and a ray from it, deeper, leaves the region through its far wall; Vex's elbow then rises smoothly from 0 to 5 cm.
  • Intersections are intersections: a review of the audit had renamed its intersecting pairs "broad-phase candidates". A two-triangle test shows Blender's overlap query is a triangle-triangle test after the box test (boxes that overlap without touching are not returned); what it cannot tell apart is contact from penetration, which is why depth is measured.

Threats that remain: the joint and hand scores compare two readings by the same pose model, so its blind spots cancel as often as they show; everything is 2D against one camera, with depth checked only by eye in the four views; there are four moves, all from generated video of the character itself, not of people; and times include waits for the GPU. Recovering a known clip from a render of it - a 3D ground truth - is the first experiment of the next phase [E101].
