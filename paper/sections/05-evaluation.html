Evaluation

Measures

We measure what a player would see, in units that do not depend on the character's size.

  • Joint error (moves from video): the character's clip is rendered at the video's own frame times from the video's camera angle; the same pose model [@vitpose] reads the video and the render, the hips are laid on each other frame by frame with one scale for the clip, and the mean distance between the twelve body joints is reported in the video's torso lengths (shoulders to hips; 0.05 is about 2.5 cm on an adult).
  • Silhouette IoU: the render's outline against the video's figure, cut out by a salient-object model [@u2net].
  • Face error: nose, eyes and ears about their centre, in torso lengths - which way the head turns.
  • Hands: DWPose [@dwpose] on both, each hand it is sure of; the 21 points about the wrist in palm lengths (mean and median), how often the palm faces the video's way, and the angle between the hands' pointing directions.
  • Per stage: the same joint error for the fitted points, the FBX written from them, the character's own skeleton, and the pose model's reading of the render - which separates the rig's error from the scorer's.
  • Four views: the clip rendered orthographically from the video's camera, the side and above, at one scale on a 10 cm grid, with each elbow's and knee's bend and each shoe's height above the floor read out per frame.
  • Foot slip: while a foot is on the floor, how far its lowest vertex slides between frames, as a share of the gait's actual nonzero ground speed, driven by the playground's own controller; absolute slip in m/s is also retained. A percentage is undefined for a stationary gait. The historical table used a 1 m/s denominator floor and is provisional below that speed; Blender's corrected v2 path and the still-legacy browser audit also need contact-mask parity before the same-schedule rerun [E029] [E122]. Sole depth remains a separate fixed-floor measure, not contact-conditioned penetration. Human perception thresholds from mannequin studies are rating-design evidence, not a universal pass value [@footskateperception].
  • Front silhouette IoU of a 3D model against its reference picture, rendered from the front.
  • Aberrations [E102]: every frame of every clip of every character, on the mesh as the viewers deform it (dual quaternion skinning), against its rest pose. Faces crushed below half their rest area or stretched past twice it; faces sheared, turned more than 60° beyond their own bone; triangles that intersect although they were more than 3 cm apart at rest [@blenderbvhtree]; penetration, how deep one body region has gone inside another, counted as deep past 2 cm; vertices under the floor; and popping, a vertex whose offset from its neighbours spikes out and back within three frames. Each is assigned to the body region of the bone that dominates the surface there. Shares are of all faces, so characters compare directly.

Aberrations

The aberration audit was checked before it was believed. A synthetic character (a box torso and an arm on two bones with a blended elbow) was built with aberrations of known size, and {{controls.passed}} of {{controls.n}} checks pass under both skinnings ([#tab-controls]): nothing where nothing moves; exactly the share of faces built to be crushed or stretched; the floor at −3 cm; a one-frame pop found on its own frame and a fast smooth bend not called one; and penetrations of 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 cm measured to the millimetre. Three of these checks failed first, and each failure was an error that would have misled the measurements of real characters ([#sec-eval-validity]). One-frame pops are only seen on the frames sampled, so the audit samples every frame; that costs a fifth more time than every second one.

On the {{aberr.n}} characters, before clearance, {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.crushed_pct}}% of faces were crushed, {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.stretched_pct}}% stretched and {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.sheared_pct}}% sheared, averaged over clips and characters. Crouch walk, the clip furthest from a standing pose, is among the two worst for every character; the other is the sprint or a strafe. Most crushed faces are out of sight: of the flagged faces, a ray along the normal escapes the character for only {{aberr.min.exposed_crushed_pct|int}}-{{aberr.max.exposed_crushed_pct|int}}% (an armpit collapses under the arm). Dual quaternion skinning, the playground's, crushes fewer faces than linear skinning, the glTF default ({{b.skinning_roster.dqs.crushed_pct}}% against {{b.skinning_roster.lbs.crushed_pct}}%), and stretches more ({{b.skinning_roster.dqs.stretched_pct}}% against {{b.skinning_roster.lbs.stretched_pct}}%): it keeps the volume that linear blending loses, and a joint bulges instead [@dqs]. Penetration was the most common aberration: in {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}% of frames, on average, one body region was more than 2 cm inside another ({{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.deep_frames_pct|int}}% counting arms through hair, which the head's region includes).

Holding the clips inside each character's joint limits ([#sec-m-clearance]) brings that to {{b.aberrations_after_clearance.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}% ({{b.aberrations_after_clearance.mean.deep_frames_pct|int}}% with hair), and solving every frame against the character's own surface ([#sec-m-contact]) then to {{aberr.mean.deep_body_frames_pct|int}}% ({{aberr.mean.deep_frames_pct|int}}% with hair); the deepest penetration between body regions, averaged over clips, goes from {{b.aberrations_before_clearance.mean.body_penetration_cm}} to {{b.aberrations_after_clearance.mean.body_penetration_cm}} and then {{aberr.mean.body_penetration_cm}} cm ([#tab-aberrations]). Clearance itself raised no character's crushed, stretched or sheared share and added no pop (one hand pop already in Wren's sprint came to straddle two frames) [E128], and the contact solve, measured alone, changed those shares by at most 0.04 points and added no pop [E151]. Against the clips after clearance, this version - the contact solve with its second look and the skin weights chosen at the extreme poses ([#sec-m-rig]) - crushes fewer faces on every character ({{b.aberrations_after_clearance.mean.crushed_pct}}% to {{aberr.mean.crushed_pct}}% on average); stretched faces rise on four characters (Juno most, 1.29% to 1.40%), sheared on six by at most 0.05 points, and pops on three (Rivet's 7 to 13, in his crouch walk and jog, not traced yet). The planted feet do not move: Bo's foot slip on walk, jog, run and sprint is identical to the fourth decimal before and after. The largest single turn clearance makes is {{aberr.min.clearance_max_deg|int}}-{{aberr.max.clearance_max_deg|int}}° per character, and armour takes the most: Cadet's knee plates meet at {{aberr.cadet.knee_limit_deg|int}}° of bend, so his sprint's swinging knee, bent {{aberr.cadet.knee_clips_max_deg|int}}° in the capture, is opened by up to {{aberr.cadet.clearance_max_deg|int}}° - a stiffer-legged sprint, plates meeting instead of passing through each other.

What clearance leaves is what a single joint's limit cannot see: hands swinging into thighs that are themselves moving (the limits are measured with the legs at rest), legs brushing past each other in strafes, arms through long hair, which is a job for the hair's own spring chains [E125], and knees through a long coat's hem, which needs the hem to follow the thighs.

Extreme poses

The 18 clips never put the arms overhead or across the chest, or squat deeply, and a neutral stance hides what skinning does there: practitioners' rigging guides ask for checks at extreme poses for that reason, and the open reference-asset-compiler [@refassetcompiler] gates its rigging on a five-pose deformation suite [E153]. blender/deform_suite.py writes ten held poses into a copy of each rig - arms overhead, forward, across the chest and behind; a deep squat; a bend with a twist; a high kick; the head turned hard; and, for the feet, on tiptoe and at heel strike, added when softer weights bled a boot's ankle in a run that the first eight could not see - each turn signed by which way it moves its child joint, so no knowledge of a rig's bone axes is needed, and the audit measures them like clips ([#tab-stress]). Skin is mostly sound at the extremes: over {{stress.n}} characters the median stretches {{stress.arms_overhead.stretched_pct.median}}% of its faces with the arms overhead and crushes {{stress.arms_across.crushed_pct.median}}% with the forearms across the chest. The tail is the finding. {{stress.arms_overhead.stretched_pct.max_name}} stretches {{stress.arms_overhead.stretched_pct.max}}% of its faces with the arms overhead, three to four times the median, and the worst for arms forward is {{stress.arms_forward.sheared_pct.max_name}} ({{stress.arms_forward.sheared_pct.max}}% sheared): the characters in loose jackets and armour, at the torso and upper arm beside the armpit, where a jacket is pulled up with the arm. Penetration at these poses is geometry, not skinning: the forearms across the chest go {{stress.arms_across.penetration_max_cm.median}} cm into a median torso, and a thicker one takes more. Shares are of all faces, so they compare only between rigs with the same face count: the face rig adds 18,196 faces to Vex's 59,617 and dilutes every share by 23%.

The battery is also what chooses each character's skin weights ([#sec-m-rig]): the default falloff and a hybrid, soft on the body and default on the legs, are rigged and posed, and the hybrid is kept when it is at least 0.1 points better on average and no pose is more than 0.3 worse ([#tab-weights]). It cannot choose alone. Two weightings it preferred - the learned weights of Make-It-Animatable [E157] and distance measured from each part's surface [E159] - held Vex's faces in shape at the extremes and in her clips let her arms sink into her jacket two to three times as often, while the second improved every clip measure of Pip's; the battery's own penetration did not tell the two apart, so the clips will have to decide ([#sec-roadmap]).

Against professionally made models

The measures above are of motion and deformation. Whether a model looks professionally made is measured by a model-quality audit run the same way on every character and on professionally made meshes [E132]: Blender Studio's Human Base Meshes [@basemeshes] and Ellie from the open film Sprite Fright [@elliepose]. Shape, on the shipped low-poly at the character's height: noise, how far each surface point's normal turns from the mean normal within 1 cm; lumps, how far the surface within 4 cm departs from the quadric that fits it best, the worst quarter left out so a designed crease does not count; triangle tilt, how far each triangle leans against the smooth normals at its corners - what a normal map has to correct; asymmetry, the mirrored surface's distance to the surface about the best mirror plane; open edges and loose pieces. Texture: lighting left in the albedo, the same garment's colour where it faces front and back (CIEDE2000), the colour step across UV seams, and the PBR range. The professional bodies score noise {{q.summary.pro_noise_deg_min|2}}-{{q.summary.pro_noise_deg_max|2}}°, lumps {{q.summary.pro_lump_mm_min|2}}-{{q.summary.pro_lump_mm_max|2}} mm and asymmetry {{q.summary.pro_symmetry_mm_min|1}}-{{q.summary.pro_symmetry_mm_max|1}} mm, with no open edges and one piece. Ellie, a layered production character, scores noise {{q.baselines.ellie.noise_deg|2}}° and lumps {{q.baselines.ellie.lump_mm|2}} mm: folds and hair are designed detail, and the audit cannot yet tell them from noise, so a garment needs comparing with garments.

[#tab-quality] scores each character's shipped model as version 0.11 built it and as rebuilt on this version's defaults - the finer surface ([#sec-m-generation]), the loose shards dropped, the hands and the skin, the albedo range ([#sec-m-texture]). Over the {{q.summary.n}} characters, the median noise went from {{q.summary.noise_deg_before|2}}° to {{q.summary.noise_deg_after|2}}°, lumps from {{q.summary.lump_mm_before|2}} to {{q.summary.lump_mm_after|2}} mm, triangle tilt from {{q.summary.tilt_deg_before|1}}° to {{q.summary.tilt_deg_after|1}}°, open edges from {{q.summary.open_per10k_before|1}} to {{q.summary.open_per10k_after|1}} per 10,000 triangles and loose pieces from {{q.summary.pieces_before}} to {{q.summary.pieces_after}}; the same garment's colour front and back from dE {{q.summary.front_back_de_before|2}} to {{q.summary.front_back_de_after|2}}. The low-poly's numbers move less than the renders do - the solid and the decimation set it - and the gap to the professional meshes remains two to four times on noise and lumps: the visible gain of the finer surface is in the normal map ([#fig-rake]).

Close-ups. A whole model's averages moved little between builds that look very different close up, so every character was rendered in eight lit close-ups - face, three-quarter, a hand from above and both hanging, a shoulder, the upper back, a foot - and each fault listed [E138] ([#fig-inventory]). On the fourteen version 0.11 builds: faces blotched where the picture is projected onto a face shaped unlike it (nine), shoulders and armpits torn by the T bake (five), two-toned hands (most), rings at bare wrists, a skin-coloured bell at Pip's wrists where the hand was cut beyond his flared cuff, dark streaks in hair, and floating debris (all). Faces, on a benchmark set

Faces are judged on a benchmark set rather than the whole roster: two characters for each style and gender - realistic Mara, Juno, Rowan and Knight, stylised Vex, Rivet, Pip and Cadet, anime Aoi, Hana, Kaito and Ren - chosen to differ (hair over the face, a helmet collar, a drawn smile, painted eyes) so a change that helps one kind and hurts another shows [E140]. Each face is rendered from the front in soft light and scored against its picture two ways. Landmark error: DWPose's 68 face points [@dwpose] on the render are fitted onto the picture's by one similarity [@umeyama] (the render is framed differently) and the mean distance left, as a share of the eye spacing, is the NME of face alignment. It is not enough: Mara's smeared, squinting version 0.12 face had every point within 3% of the picture's, because a smeared eye is still where an eye should be. Likeness: the render is laid onto the picture by those points and the whole face, the eyes, the nose and the mouth are compared by the cosine of their DINOv3 features [@dinov3], robust to the render's light but not to a smeared lid. Over the {{f.summary.like_eyes_paired_n}} characters with a version 0.12 build to compare, the median likeness of the eyes went from {{f.summary.like_eyes_before|2}} to {{f.summary.like_eyes_after_paired|2}} ({{f.summary.like_eyes_better_n}} better), of the whole face from {{f.summary.like_face_before|2}} to {{f.summary.like_face_after_paired|2}} ({{f.summary.like_face_better_n}} better), of the mouth from {{f.summary.like_mouth_before|2}} to {{f.summary.like_mouth_after_paired|2}} ({{f.summary.like_mouth_better_n}} better), and the landmark error from {{f.summary.nme_before|pct1}} to {{f.summary.nme_after_paired|pct1}} ([#tab-faces], [#fig-faces-bench]). The gain is the realistic faces': their eyes from {{f.summary.realistic_like_eyes_before|2}} to {{f.summary.realistic_like_eyes_after|2}} and whole faces from {{f.summary.realistic_like_face_before|2}} to {{f.summary.realistic_like_face_after|2}}, where every part had been misplaced and the eyes were pits. Stylised faces held ({{f.summary.stylized_like_face_before|2}} and {{f.summary.stylized_like_face_after|2}}) and so did anime ({{f.summary.anime_like_face_before|2}} and {{f.summary.anime_like_face_after|2}}): drawn features were already roughly where the generator sculpts them, one of the eight cannot be read on the model at all (Aoi's eyes are drawn too large; Vex's could not be until her model was made again without the second face on the back of her head, E145), and the faces left wrong are wrong in shape - Ren's head longer than his picture's. On the same characters the rest of the model held: median surface noise {{f.summary.noise_deg_v012|2}}° in version 0.12 and {{f.summary.noise_deg_v013|2}}° now, asymmetry {{f.summary.symmetry_mm_v012|1}} and {{f.summary.symmetry_mm_v013|1}} mm, faces crushed during the clips {{f.summary.crushed_pct_v012|2}}% and {{f.summary.crushed_pct_v013|2}}%, foot slip {{f.summary.slip_v012|1}}% and {{f.summary.slip_v013|1}}% of the ground speed; frames with a part deep in another moved by {{f.summary.deep_change_min|1}} to +{{f.summary.deep_change_max|1}} points a character (a mean of {{f.summary.deep_change_mean|1}}): the largest, Vex's, is her arms 2-5 cm into her torso in most clips, at the audit's 2 cm line, and her first rebuild of this round had scored 45.9% against version 0.12's 46.4%.

Checks a person would make

Two faults shipped that a person saw at a glance and no measure caught: five characters without a right hand, and one with a face on the back of her head. Both are now measured - every modelled fingertip looked for on the low-poly, and a face read on the back of the head [E145] [E146] - and close-ups of every character's hands, back and back of head are rendered for review. Validity of the measurements

Several of the largest errors we found were in the measuring, not the pipeline. We list them because each would have sent the work in the wrong direction.

  • The comparison ran fast [E010]. The side-by-side and four-view videos resampled the 24 fps video to the renders' 30 fps by taking each video frame once, never twice, so the video played 25% fast and then stood still: the rig seemed to punch 0.2 s late, more as the clip went on. The fidelity score was unaffected, since it renders the rig at the video's own times.
  • The scorer misread the render [E011]. Mara's kick scored {{kick.joints}}, with a dip to 0.46 at the top of the kick that read as the rig folding its leg. Held against the rig's own skeleton, the pose model's reading of the render was wrong in {{kick.misread_n}} frames (the skeleton was 0.12-0.14 from the video there, as elsewhere); without them the kick scores {{kick.joints_trusted}}. The four views confirm the leg is at head height.
  • Face positions in stairs [E019]: {{b.face_quantisation.before_mm}} mm quantisation from a half-float render target, found while tracing an unrelated seam.
  • Stale caches: the per-stage tool kept its skeleton dumps forever and was scoring a rig two rebuilds old.
  • Triangles that change under the audit [E102]: Blender splits a quad along its shorter diagonal in its current shape, so the synthetic elbow's quads re-split at 55° of bend and a comparison with rest compared different triangles. The audit now splits once, at rest.
  • Depth that stopped at 3 cm: the first penetration search looked a fixed six vertex rings from the intersection and measured a 5 cm push as 3.1 cm. It now floods through every vertex that is still inside.
  • Inside an open tube: a body region is an open patch (the upper arm is a tube open at both ends), and past its open end the nearest face's normal says nothing about inside. Aoi's bent forearm read 15.9 cm inside her upper arm; Vex's elbow read 15.7 cm at 105° of bend and 0 at 115°. A vertex now counts as inside only if it projects straight onto the face and a ray from it, deeper, leaves the region through its far wall; Vex's elbow then rises smoothly from 0 to 5 cm.
  • Intersections are intersections: a review of the audit had renamed its intersecting pairs "broad-phase candidates". A two-triangle test shows Blender's overlap query is a triangle-triangle test after the box test (boxes that overlap without touching are not returned); what it cannot tell apart is contact from penetration, which is why depth is measured.
  • Planting that switched itself off [E129]: foot planting needs the capture's toe bones, UniMate's pruned skeleton ends at them, and the FBX importer drops a final bone as a leaf. Nothing failed: the generated moves were simply never planted, and stood 3-19 cm into the floor, sliding. Retargeting now says so when it cannot plant.
  • Planting the wrong surface [E130]: the planted sole was a rigid copy of the shoe carried on the ankle bone, and the audit measured the mesh. On an ordinary shoe the two agree; Cadet's boot blends with the shin above a high ankle, and the solve kept the copy on the floor while the boot on screen went 4.4 cm under it, in 48% of his frames. Posed by their own skin weights, with linear blending the points still sat a few centimetres above the boot the viewer draws; with dual quaternions, as the viewer skins, they match Blender's deformation within 0.01 mm.

Threats that remain: the joint and hand scores compare two readings by the same pose model, so its blind spots cancel as often as they show; everything is 2D against one camera, with depth checked only by eye in the four views; there are four moves, all from generated video of the character itself, not of people; and times include waits for the GPU. Recovering a known clip from a render of it - a 3D ground truth - is the first experiment of the next phase [E101].
