Limitations and open problems

  • Fused layers. Hair, braids, bags and layered garments are one surface with what they lie on. Chains on them tear the surface, so Aoi's hair and Mara's braid are rigid, and between Pip's vest and sleeve the armpit stretches with his arms overhead [E110].
  • A sleeve past a bent elbow. Linear skinning keeps a jacket sleeve that extends past the elbow pointing along the upper arm when the elbow bends far; in Aoi's spell cast the cuffs stick out sideways. Pose-space correctives would reach it [E107].
  • Joins after the arm cut. 1-3 joined slabs an arm survive the re-cut on several characters; the rig keeps that side of the garment with the arm [E026].
  • Faces. Faces the stage cannot read get no rig (Bo) [E109]. Kept from the generator where the reference cannot be laid on them, realistic faces are softer than the picture and the fringe carries the generator's colour (Knight) [E022].
  • Depth in moves from video is borrowed from the nearest capture, so a move unlike anything in the library is right in the image and approximate in depth [E108]; the pose model loses poses it rarely saw, on video and on render [E011].
  • Measurement. No independent 3D ground truth for recovered motion yet; current end-to-end motion evidence is four generated clips. The aberration audit finds defects of known size on a synthetic character, but its scores are not yet calibrated against blinded reviewers, so none is used as a gate [E101] [E102]. Its penetration depth assumes each body region is a closed-ish surface: a limb buried whole inside another, with no triangle crossing, is not seen, and a face ray escaping along its normal is a geometric proxy for being in view, not a camera's. Foot slip is now measured against each gait's true ground speed in Blender; the playground's own readout still divides by at least 1 m/s and tracks contact differently, so the two remain different estimands until they are reconciled, and the geometric 4 mm contact test has not been checked against pressure-insole labels [E122]. Real-video motion and scan-anchored synthetic-view evaluation remain planned [E111] [E113].
  • Generated geometry and texture. Solidification has no source-to-output shape-preservation benchmark [E116]; retopology has no sampled surface-error score [E118]; UV checks can miss local stretch despite a good median density [E119]; and texture gutters have not been checked through the mip levels and samplers used by target engines [E120]. Part-parser confidence is discarded before label fusion, so its errors cannot yet be calibrated or abstained on [E117].
  • Prompt-to-asset fidelity. Prompt expansion has rule checks and the model has a front silhouette gate, but the evaluation does not track requested attributes through the reference image into the final character [E112]. In particular, held props, action/pose requests and numeric height are currently unsupported or omitted by the structured description; short prompts also need a separate passthrough check.
  • Independent evaluation. “Held-out” is not yet backed by a frozen subject/sequence/prompt manifest or duplicate-group rule, and repeated frames/views/seeds must not be counted as independent samples. Human quality ratings need blinded, score-boundary examples and reported inter-rater reliability before any proxy is used as a gate [E101] [E102] [E111] [E112] [E121].
  • Engine delivery. The README documents Unity, Unreal and Godot setup, and the paper lists glTF/FBX outputs; version-pinned importer/runtime smoke results are not yet in the experiment record. FBX named LODs and glTF LOD0 have different export shapes, and clip grouping may vary by importer; the manifest does not prove that the optimized web assets or release ZIPs carry every advertised feature. glTF conformance alone will not establish engine behavior [E114].
  • Speed. A character takes about {{stage.total.min}} minutes of stage time, a move 35-50 minutes of video generation; most of the former is two 3D passes and two image-model calls [E106].
