Roadmap

The next phase aims at three things at once: more precise measurement, fewer visible aberrations, and less time per character. The new checks separate known truth, model-based proxies, human acceptance, and engine behavior. Each item is an experiment in the tracker with its metric fixed before it runs.

Where the gaps come from. A self-examination of every measurement over the 17 characters [E148] put the largest gaps at the surface (noise and lumps 3.4-4.7 times a professional body's), the motion (27% of animation frames with a body part more than 2 cm inside another - arms and hands in the torso and thighs) and realistic faces (likeness to their picture 0.65), and traced most of the faults found since to one root: the generator makes a single fused surface, and eleven of the pipeline's repairs exist to recover structure it never had, each with failures of its own - missing hands, holes in backs, rings round eyes, lines across mouths. Two root-level changes follow, both from published, openly licensed work. A template body under every character [E149]: Anny [@anny], a parametric body with a professional topology, finger and eye joints, UVs and the 52 ARKit facial shapes, fitted under the character in seconds; on Mara its body, wrapped onto her tight clothing, measures surface noise 0.65° and lumps 0.26 mm - in the professional range - and its face, shaped through its own face changes by 68 landmarks, puts her features within 4.5 mm of her model's, where wrapping the face vertex by vertex copied the generated face's faults. What lies outside the body becomes clothing and hair layers: the template fits all six characters tried, stylised and anime builds included (joints within 1 cm, bare skin ~3 mm), takes the character's paint in its own UVs, and with the parser's labels the jacket, trousers, boots and hair come away from the unclothed fit as one layer over the painted body ([#fig-template]), whose 52 facial actions and painted eyes work on Mara's head ([#fig-template-face]). On her rig, with the template's weights folded onto CharForge's bones, it animates worse than the shipped model (crushed faces 0.87% to 1.75%, deep frames 7.5% to 17% on 18 clips) - smooth, tuned weights matter more than topology; rigged by CharForge's own stages instead - garments keeping their own weights, the body hidden wherever the layer covers it, and the contact solve run - and its hand bones taken from the template's own fingers - it has the fewest intersecting surfaces of any version (189 a frame against the shipped 223) and as little stretch (1.04% against 1.03%), with a few more crushed faces (0.90% against 0.86%), pops (5 against 3) and deep frames (5% against 0.25%). On stylised Pip the same chain has fewer deep frames than the shipped Pip (9.0% against 11.2%) and, once its hands are fitted to his modelled knuckles and fingertips, fewer intersecting surfaces (411 against 593), but hands that still deform worse than his modelled ones (pops 31 against 13). It is not in the build yet. Retargeting that sees the body: MeshRet [@meshret], which models the dense interactions between surfaces as it retargets, runs on the laptop but, measured on six characters [E150], is no better than clearance and blind to hands in thighs; its idea, solved directly per clip against the character's own surface [E151] ([#sec-m-contact]), took the roster's frames with a part more than 2 cm inside another from 30% to 8.5% on average (Pip's from 59% to 11%), and now runs in every build.

  1. Shape and texture as clean as a professional's [E132]: the audit and its baselines now exist, and this version spent the first round on it - the finer surface [E135], the loose shards, the hands and the skin [E138], the rest pose [E137] ([#sec-eval-quality]). What is left, in the order a viewer sees it: faces - taken part by part in version 0.13 (eyes rebuilt, features laid on the model's own, the face the picture's alone [E140]); next the hairline, anime eyes the pose model cannot read, the profile, and relief from the picture's estimated normals where it is still missing [@hi3dgen] [E136], with a head made on its own as the route to a sculpted shape [E139]; hair without the dark streaks where views disagree; armour raised above the shoulder, with rigid shoulder pieces [E110]; the gap of two to four times on surface noise and lumps, which the low-poly's decimation now sets (crease-aware normals, a quad remesh of the solid); and likeness measured beside cleanliness on the whole body as it now is on faces (per-part DINOv3 likeness, [#sec-eval-faces]), since the texture audit scored Hunyuan3D-Paint's views better on characters where they were plainly worse [E134].
  2. A 3D ground truth for motion [E101]: render a character performing a known clip as a video, run the move lane on it with that clip held out of the library, and measure the recovered clip in joint angles, positions, contacts and jitter. Then test independent real footage and subjects [E111]: TotalCapture and 3DPW supply different video/pose evidence; AMASS supplies varied motion for exact-GT renders, while AIST++ is a reconstructed-motion stress set.
  3. An aberration score, calibrated against people [E102]: the audit now measures every frame of every clip and finds known-size defects to the millimetre ([#sec-eval-aberr]); what it lacks is agreement with human judgement. Blinded, randomised ratings of clips near each score's boundary, by at least two raters, before any score becomes a gate; dynamic-human quality studies seed the taxonomy, not a transferable scorer [@mate3d; @ddhqa; @dhqa4d]. Texture drift and face-morph deformation need their own measures.
  4. What the contact solve does not reach [E128, E151]: the arms are now solved against the torso and legs every frame ([#sec-m-contact]); the legs are not - a thigh into a belly in a crouch, shins passing in a strafe - and the moves made from video carry penetrations of 9-11 cm that small turns cannot undo; colliders for the hair's spring chains, so arms push long hair aside instead of through it [E125]; long coat hems that follow the thighs; and corrective shapes at elbows and shoulders, scored by the same audit [E107].
  5. Moves from words without a person choosing [E129]: rank UniMate's samples by the audits (E102's aberrations, foot slip) and by how well a render reads as the sentence, so the best of three is picked automatically; write the user's words into the caption style itself; and use UniMate's other two abilities - in-betweening, to join one clip to the next, and editing with chosen joints held, to give a captured walk a new upper-body action without touching its planted feet.
  6. Prompt-to-asset fidelity [E112]: use per-attribute questions, informed by TIFA and GenEval [@tifa; @geneval], to find where a held-out prompt's attributes are lost or changed between its structured text, reference, and final character.
  7. 3D and held-out-view fidelity [E113]: compare a permission-checked scan set against generated geometry and reserved synthetic orbit renders. Do not call these captured calibrated views; obtain written permission for external sharing of derived THUman data or keep the measurements internal [@thuman2; @lpips].
  8. Rigging against the field [E103]: our joints and weights against UniRig and the hosted auto-riggers on the same meshes, scored by [E102]; corrective shapes at elbows and shoulders [E107].
  9. Engine acceptance and provenance [E114] [E115]: run the glTF Validator [@gltfvalidator], then smoke-import representative release artifacts in version-pinned Unity, Unreal and Godot projects [@unityimport; @unrealimport; @godotimport]; report clip coverage, manifest-driven controller error, morph/spring/LOD behavior, runtime costs, exact model/data versions and terms without guessing at permissions.
  10. Time [E106]: each stage timed alone, and the costly ones - the second 3D pass, the detailed face - ablated against their measured benefit; Hunyuan3D 2.1 against TRELLIS.2 on our references [E104].
  11. Texture seams by Poisson blending [E105]; depth for moves the library lacks from a world-grounded video model [E108]; faces the rig will not guess [E109]; hair and garments as their own layers [E110].

The newly identified asset-stage gaps are tracked separately so their measurements can proceed in parallel after the shared data contracts are frozen: solidification topology versus shape fidelity and segmentation confidence [E116] [E117]; retopology distance, local UV distortion and runtime mip bleed [E118] [E119] [E120]; 3D hand, expressive-face and independent-layer quality [E123] [E124] [E125]. The evidence ledger, corrected foot metric and Studio state-machine acceptance are cross-cutting work [E121] [E122] [E126]. The detailed split manifest, artifact paths, formulas and run dependencies remain in the tracker and the development-facing research/GAPS.md. The fast classifier proposed in a user-supplied video is queued as [E127] at the very end; its identity and license remain unverified, and it is gated on completion or explicit deferral of the earlier queue.
