Roadmap

The next phase aims at three things at once: more precise measurement, fewer visible aberrations, and less time per character. The new checks separate known truth, model-based proxies, human acceptance, and engine behavior. Each item is an experiment in the tracker with its metric fixed before it runs.

  1. Shape and texture as clean as a professional's [E132]: first measure what separates our models from professionally made characters - surface roughness where a surface should be smooth, soft creases, topology faults, lighting left in the albedo, a front that does not match the back, smeared sides and back, seams - on the roster and on professional baselines, with the thresholds fixed first. Then aim a change at each gap and score it on the same audit: the reference's lighting removed before it is projected [E133]; sides and back painted by a multi-view PBR texture model [E134] [@hunyuan3d21]; quad remeshing with the generated detail baked back, and feature-preserving smoothing [E135]; detail from estimated normals [@hi3dgen] [E136]; seams [E105]; another generator [E104].
  2. A 3D ground truth for motion [E101]: render a character performing a known clip as a video, run the move lane on it with that clip held out of the library, and measure the recovered clip in joint angles, positions, contacts and jitter. Then test independent real footage and subjects [E111]: TotalCapture and 3DPW supply different video/pose evidence; AMASS supplies varied motion for exact-GT renders, while AIST++ is a reconstructed-motion stress set.
  3. An aberration score, calibrated against people [E102]: the audit now measures every frame of every clip and finds known-size defects to the millimetre ([#sec-eval-aberr]); what it lacks is agreement with human judgement. Blinded, randomised ratings of clips near each score's boundary, by at least two raters, before any score becomes a gate; dynamic-human quality studies seed the taxonomy, not a transferable scorer [@mate3d; @ddhqa; @dhqa4d]. Texture drift and face-morph deformation need their own measures.
  4. What clearance cannot see [E128]: limits that follow a moving limb, so a hand stays out of a thigh that is itself swinging and legs pass each other in a strafe; colliders for the hair's spring chains, so arms push long hair aside instead of through it [E125]; long coat hems that follow the thighs; and corrective shapes at elbows and shoulders, scored by the same audit [E107].
  5. Moves from words without a person choosing [E129]: rank UniMate's samples by the audits (E102's aberrations, foot slip) and by how well a render reads as the sentence, so the best of three is picked automatically; write the user's words into the caption style itself; and use UniMate's other two abilities - in-betweening, to join one clip to the next, and editing with chosen joints held, to give a captured walk a new upper-body action without touching its planted feet.
  6. Prompt-to-asset fidelity [E112]: use per-attribute questions, informed by TIFA and GenEval [@tifa; @geneval], to find where a held-out prompt's attributes are lost or changed between its structured text, reference, and final character.
  7. 3D and held-out-view fidelity [E113]: compare a permission-checked scan set against generated geometry and reserved synthetic orbit renders. Do not call these captured calibrated views; obtain written permission for external sharing of derived THUman data or keep the measurements internal [@thuman2; @lpips].
  8. Rigging against the field [E103]: our joints and weights against UniRig and the hosted auto-riggers on the same meshes, scored by [E102]; corrective shapes at elbows and shoulders [E107].
  9. Engine acceptance and provenance [E114] [E115]: run the glTF Validator [@gltfvalidator], then smoke-import representative release artifacts in version-pinned Unity, Unreal and Godot projects [@unityimport; @unrealimport; @godotimport]; report clip coverage, manifest-driven controller error, morph/spring/LOD behavior, runtime costs, exact model/data versions and terms without guessing at permissions.
  10. Time [E106]: each stage timed alone, and the costly ones - the second 3D pass, the detailed face - ablated against their measured benefit; Hunyuan3D 2.1 against TRELLIS.2 on our references [E104].
  11. Texture seams by Poisson blending [E105]; depth for moves the library lacks from a world-grounded video model [E108]; faces the rig will not guess [E109]; hair and garments as their own layers [E110].

The newly identified asset-stage gaps are tracked separately so their measurements can proceed in parallel after the shared data contracts are frozen: solidification topology versus shape fidelity and segmentation confidence [E116] [E117]; retopology distance, local UV distortion and runtime mip bleed [E118] [E119] [E120]; 3D hand, expressive-face and independent-layer quality [E123] [E124] [E125]. The evidence ledger, corrected foot metric and Studio state-machine acceptance are cross-cutting work [E121] [E122] [E126]. The detailed split manifest, artifact paths, formulas and run dependencies remain in the tracker and the development-facing research/GAPS.md. The fast classifier proposed in a user-supplied video is queued as [E127] at the very end; its identity and license remain unverified, and it is gated on completion or explicit deferral of the earlier queue.
