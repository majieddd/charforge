Appendix D · Archive

Measurements and methods this paper used to report, kept as they were with the reason each was replaced. The lab notebook keeps the longer record, with its own archive at the end.

Foot slip with a 1 m/s floor under the speed [E029]

The first foot audit divided each gait's contact slip by max(manifest speed, 1 m/s): values at or above 1 m/s were normalised by the stated speed, values below 1 m/s were understated, and percentages for standing still are undefined, so slow entries were left out. On it the worse foot slid a median {{feet_e029.median_slip_pct}}% of ground speed, at worst {{feet_e029.max_slip_pct}}%, over {{feet_e029.n}} character-gait pairs. It was replaced by the true-speed audit of [#sec-eval-feet], rerun on every character; the old table is frozen in research/data/e029_feet_archived.json. Note that it also predates the rigid sole's replacement [E130].

The generated surface at 200,000 faces, unfiltered [E135]

Until version 0.11 TRELLIS.2 simplified its surface to 200,000 faces, and the solid, the low-poly and the normal map were built on it as it came. Large flat facets on gently curved armour and sawtooth plate edges reached the normal map as a triangle pattern under a grazing light. Replaced by 1M faces and bilateral normal filtering ([#sec-m-generation]); the v0.11 scores of every character are the left side of [#tab-quality].

The T baked into the rest pose [E137]

Until version 0.11 the rig was posed from the generated A-pose to a T, a copy of its armature modifier applied to the mesh, and the posed skeleton made the rest - so that Mixamo's rest and the target's agreed. The linear blending that carries the mesh up to the T cannot be undone by the same blending on the way down: on Cadet it sheared the pauldrons into shards and left slivers in every arms-down frame. Replaced by the T recorded on the armature and used only in the retargeting maths ([#sec-m-rig]); --rest T still builds the old way.

Modelled hands shaded from the colour bake [E138]

The modelled hands took the face's skin tone with ±12% of light and dark from the base colour bake under them - a bake of the generated mesh, where the modelled hand did not exist - which painted pale and dark patches across most hands. Replaced by shading from the ambient occlusion baked on the solid that contains them.

The face laid on by optical flow, or kept from the generator [E022] [E140]

Until version 0.12 the face was laid on through a finer flow of its own, matched on an upscaled crop on lightness for the eyes and brows and on redness for the lips, and refused when it would move features more than 2.5% of the figure's height - on drawn faces a larger warp painted a second pair of eyes. Knight's generated face sat 2.5% from his picture's: refused, the picture went on unaligned, his fringe across one eye and every feature doubled; forced, the eyes smeared into bands. For realistic characters whose face the flow refused, the front view left the face and fringe to the generator's own texture, softer but on its geometry ([#fig-knight-face]). Accepted, the flow still squinted and smeared Mara's eyes. Replaced by the face laid on in parts ([#sec-m-texture]); both remain in the code as the fallback when the model's face cannot be read.

One skin tone over the face [E138]

In version 0.12 the skin tone evened the face too, to take the picture's shading off a shape not quite the picture's. With the face laid on in parts the shading lies on the right shape, and the tone's colour bands cut the picture's soft cheek shadow into patches; since version 0.13 the face is left as painted.

A rigid sole on the ankle bone

Until version 0.11 the planted foot was a copy of the sole - the shoe's vertices within 3 cm of the floor at rest, at most 160 - carried rigidly on the ankle bone. It is right for a shoe that is rigid below the ankle and wrong for one that blends with the shin, as [#sec-eval-feet] measures.
