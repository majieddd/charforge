# CharForge research gap review

**Reviewed 2026-09-26.** This is a development-facing companion to the paper and experiment tracker. It reads the current README, runbook, limitations, paper, and tracker as the project's evidence—not as proof that every stated behavior has been independently revalidated here. New experiments below are proposals, not completed results. The tracker remains the source of truth for status.

## Main finding

CharForge has unusually good failure-driven instrumentation for a small project: it separates motion stages, checks a build from four views, keeps an experiment history, and fixes known character-specific failures rather than hiding them. The largest remaining gap is evidential coverage. Many pipeline rules have been measured on a small set of generated characters, while the final claims concern arbitrary prompts, true 3D motion, unseen views, deformation, and several game engines.

Keep three kinds of evidence distinct:

1. **Reference truth:** a known scan, mocap sequence, calibrated camera, or manually labelled sample.
2. **Proxy score:** a pose detector, segmentation model, silhouette overlap, or perceptual image metric. Useful for triage, but it can share the pipeline's blind spots.
3. **Human acceptance:** whether a reviewer finds the character faithful, well-shaped, well-textured, and plausible in motion. It needs a controlled rubric and more than one dimension.

Do not collapse these into one quality number until the proxy has been calibrated against the other two.

## Stage-by-stage gaps and evidence

### 1. Prompt, specification, and reference image

**What is established.** E013 compares two image models on six characters; E015 evaluates nine prompt models on five short prompts with 20 rule checks; E028 catches references whose arms are too close to the body. The Studio exposes the expanded text for editing.

**Gap.** These checks measure prompt formatting and a few reference-image defects, not whether the requested attributes survive the whole chain into the final character. `pipeline/describe.py` also rejects or omits held props, action/pose requests and numeric height, while the prompt path treats short descriptions differently near its 20-word passthrough boundary. A prompt can appear accepted even when a requested feature is not represented downstream.

**Evidence and use.** [TIFA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Hu_TIFA_Accurate_and_Interpretable_Text-to-Image_Faithfulness_Evaluation_with_Question_Answering_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf) shows how per-prompt questions expose attribute omissions that holistic image-text scores hide. [GenEval](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/a3bf71c7c63f0c3bcb7ff67c67b1e7b1-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html) separates object count, co-occurrence, position, and colour. Both are image-generation benchmarks, not validated judges of stylised 3D characters; adapt the *question design*, and audit its answers with people.

**Next measure.** [E112](experiments.json) freezes 12 prompt IDs, supported/unsupported attributes, prompt lengths and seeds, then records the structured description, reference and eight fixed orbit views. Score correct route/refusal separately from silent loss for unsupported features; report prompt-level macro precision/recall, property/hand association and height error in cm when a numeric target exists. Two seeds are nested repeats, not independent prompts.

### 2. 3D generation, multi-view consistency, and appearance

**What is established.** E014 gates the first pass by front silhouette IoU and successfully rejects several backdrop-as-geometry failures. E012 found only a small turntable IoU gain from extra conditioning views, with the braid averaged away; that is a useful negative result, but not an accuracy measurement against known 3D shape. E104 already plans a same-reference generator comparison; keep it paired by prompt/settings/device, add blind alignment/geometry/texture ratings, and call silhouette IoU a proxy rather than 3D truth.

**Gap.** A good front silhouette does not establish correct side/back shape, watertightness, or texture agreement. Generated turntable frames are not independent ground truth when the tested generator may be responsible for their content. Current generator comparisons can rank outputs, but cannot say which is geometrically closer to a known character.

**Evidence and use.** [MATE-3D](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zhang_Benchmarking_and_Learning_Multi-Dimensional_Quality_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) collects separate human ratings for prompt alignment, geometry, texture, and overall quality; the dimensions do not always move together. THUman provides scan meshes/textures and recipes for rendering RGB/normal/depth, but its public README does not establish captured calibrated multi-view RGB observations; any reserved orbit views are synthetic renders, not captured-camera truth. Pin the exact THUman release (the current repo also describes THUman2.1), and obtain written permission before sharing scan-derived data with third parties; otherwise keep the experiment internal. The scans are realistic-human-only and do not represent anime or fantasy designs. [LPIPS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Zhang_The_Unreasonable_Effectiveness_CVPR_2018_paper.html) is an image metric, not a geometry or rigging score.

**Next measure.** [E113](experiments.json) uses a small, permission-checked scan set, creates a front input and reserved synthetic orbit views, and reports area-sampled shape distance separately from rendered-view appearance. For prediction samples P and scan mesh M_GT, report `CD_H = (mean_p d(p,M_GT) + mean_q d(q,M_pred))/(2H)`, both directed `D95/H` values, and raw mm; validate with known translation/scale perturbations. Align with declared camera/floor/height and no per-output best-fit scale. Keep derived files private absent written permission and results research-only/style-scoped.

### 3. Part labels, joints, solidification, and mesh repair

**What is established.** The system renders eight views, back-projects parser labels, estimates joints, closes the generated shell, traces limbs, and cuts arms/hands free. The paper documents failures and repairs in E018, E025, E026, and E027. The remaining arm-cut joins are explicitly open in E026.

**Gap.** I did not find a checked-in, manually labelled cross-style gold set for part boundaries, joint centers, or cut surfaces in the research materials reviewed. The parser's architecture paper does not validate the particular checkpoint on generated anime, armor, hair, or layered clothing. A plausible front render can conceal label leakage, disconnected solids, or surviving bridges.

**Next measure.** [E116](experiments.json) pairs solidification input/output and injects holes, leaks, nested shells and shards; report watertight/manifold success separately from volume, surface-distance and silhouette preservation. [E117](experiments.json) retains parser probabilities and view weights, then compares hard unit votes with soft consensus/abstention on a manually labeled held-out set. Report area-weighted class IoU, boundary F1 at a frozen physical tolerance, area-weighted calibration error, and error versus accepted-area; tune abstention only on character-disjoint calibration data. CVPR calibration and reliability studies show confidence, correctness and domain shift must be checked, but provide no ready CharForge threshold. Keep the difficult cross-style gold set, joint-tip/centerline error, component counts and cut bridges in the protocol; retain thumbnails.

### 4. Retopology, UVs, texture projection, and material maps

**What is established.** E027 checks UV-island overlap; E020 and E022 address skin-colour and face-projection failures; E105 plans a Poisson-blending comparison. Textures are baked as several engine-specific maps.

**Gap.** The current seam metric is a local colour step and retained high-frequency detail; it does not test source-to-retopo shape deviation, local UV Jacobian distortion, runtime mip bleed, normal-map orientation, material packing, or repeatability under fixed lighting/camera. A good island-overlap count and median texel density can hide a severely stretched local region.

**Evidence and use.** The [MATE-3D human study](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zhang_Benchmarking_and_Learning_Multi-Dimensional_Quality_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) supports rating texture separately from geometry and alignment. [LPIPS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Zhang_The_Unreasonable_Effectiveness_CVPR_2018_paper.html) can compare fixed, held-out views as a secondary signal. Existing [Poisson Image Editing](https://doi.org/10.1145/882262.882269) is directly relevant to E105, but the fact another system uses it does not show it will preserve CharForge's detail.

**Next measure.** [E118](experiments.json) reports directed mean and 95th-percentile area-sampled source-to-retopo distance normalized by character height, normal-angle deviation and fixed-view silhouettes without rescaling; this is preservation of the input surface, not truth against an intended character. [E119](experiments.json) measures the UV-map Jacobian's anisotropy and area-weighted density percentiles by region. [E120](experiments.json) compares chart-boundary samples at every exported mip: ΔE00 for color, decoded-normal angular error, and per-channel absolute ORM error under fixed runtime samplers. Continue E105 with fixed exposure/light/cameras; store raw images and per-view results.

### 5. Hands, faces, layered hair/garments, and skin deformation

**What is established.** DWPose hands inform E006; E017/E019/E022 cover face detection and projection; E020/E021 improve hand colour/fit; E026 and E110 document fused layers. E107 plans corrective shapes. The five face shapes and generated hands have targeted checks, but not a broad expressive-performance benchmark.

**Gap.** Static landmark success is not the same as expressive range or stable deformation. E006 scores generated-video hand pose, but the tracker has little external 3D hand truth and does not test hand-shape changes at extreme finger poses. Face acceptance currently centers on finding landmarks and building a rig; it does not yet score whether blinks, jaw, smile, brow, and pucker preserve silhouette/texture across styles. Hair and clothes fused into one shell limit secondary motion.

**Evidence and use.** [MANO](https://mano.is.tue.mpg.de/) learns pose-conditioned hand-shape corrections from scans and [SMPL-X](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Pavlakos_Expressive_Body_Capture_3D_Hands_Face_and_Body_From_a_Single_Image_CVPR_2019_paper.html) treats hands, face, and body as coordinated expressive regions with richer image cues and pose priors. [AU-Blendshape (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Li_AU-Blendshape_for_Fine-grained_Stylized_3D_Facial_Expression_Manipulation_ICCV_2025_paper.html) specifically studies stylized AU controls and offers a more relevant expression-challenge taxonomy; its dataset/model are not a CharForge benchmark or drop-in rig, and their terms must be checked before reuse. These sources justify region-specific checks; they are not templates for arbitrary CharForge meshes. [Pose Space Deformation](https://doi.org/10.1145/344779.344862) is a principled basis for pose-conditioned correctives, but does not establish which correctives will work on these generated garments.

For dynamic quality, [DDH-QA (ICME 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12734) studies human ratings for binding errors and motion unnaturalness over ten motion types. [DHQA-4D (Pattern Recognition, 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2026.114567) includes geometry, texture, compression, and temporal-discontinuity distortions with human ratings on scanned 4D humans. Both help seed E102's challenge taxonomy; their learned scorers and scanned-real-human distributions are not assumed to transfer to stylized generated meshes.

**Next measure.** [E123](experiments.json) separates hand-detector error, retargeted 3D joint error and surface appearance on a licensed, frozen InterHand2.6M subset; first confirm whether its published split is actually subject-held-out, otherwise make and document a permitted subject/sequence holdout. Report wrist-rooted palm-normalized joint error and joint-angle error separately from global wrist position, and test whether the local wrist-trust score predicts held-out error reduction; 3D joints are not surface truth. [E124](experiments.json) tests existing face controls at 0/0.5/1 and through transitions; use D3DFACS expert AU labels only where supplied and FaMoS as dynamic timing/scan evidence, not AU-labeled target truth. Report feature-gap change normalized by interocular distance, expression intent, likeness, clipping and temporal smoothness separately. After E110 creates independent layers, [E125](experiments.json) measures attachment displacement, penetration, tearing and settling under abrupt motions, with blinded plausibility ratings to catch both insufficient and excessive swing. Keep E107/E109/E110 open until multiple styles pass.

### 6. Skeletons, weights, and animation clips

**What is established.** E001–E008 improve retrieval, fitting, retargeting, hands, elbows, and grounding. E024 adds dual-quaternion skinning in the three.js playground; E029 scores locomotion foot slip. E103 compares against UniRig and hosted auto-riggers; E107 addresses known elbow/shoulder artifacts.

**Gap.** Most end-to-end motion results use four generated clips of the character itself; the self-tests provide controlled evidence but are not independent-subject generalization. The archived foot-slip ratio divides by `max(speed,1 m/s)`, understating the reported share below 1 m/s and making stationary percentages undefined. The rig comparison also lacks a frozen subject/sequence split, exact coordinate/filter definitions and independent hand truth.

**Evidence and use.** Existing [RigNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00559) and [UniRig](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12451) are the relevant published rig baselines. Use E103's same-mesh/same-pose setup, but report joint error and dynamic deformation as different outcomes; score E107 through E102 rather than bone agreement alone.

### 7. Motion from video and depth recovery

**What is established.** The current lane retrieves from 2,453 Mixamo motions, fits to 2D observations, retargets, refines the character's mesh, and checks four generated moves. E101 already proposes an exact synthetic ground truth; E108 compares world-grounded recovery on that ground truth.

**Gap.** Synthetic rendering isolates algorithm error, but still will not establish robustness to real video, camera movement, clothing, occlusion, or pose-estimator failure. Conversely, real-video benchmarks vary in label quality and coordinate frames, so they should not be pooled as though they were equivalent ground truth.

**Evidence and use.** [AMASS](https://amass.is.tue.mpg.de/) combines 15 optical-mocap sources and supplies broad rigged motion, but no paired RGB. [TotalCapture](https://cvssp.org/projects/totalcapture/TotalCapture/) pairs synchronized calibrated RGB, IMU, and Vicon labels (about 1.9 million frames), but has only five participants in a controlled indoor volume. [3DPW](https://virtualhumans.mpi-inf.mpg.de/3DPW/) brings moving-phone-camera footage and IMU/video-derived poses into the wild; report root-relative pose separately from global trajectory and document alignment. [AIST++](https://google.github.io/aistplusplus_dataset/factsfigures.html) adds 1,408 dance sequences across ten genres and nine views, but its 3D motion is reconstructed from multi-view footage; use it as a diversity stress set, honor its split and license, and exclude its published low-quality annotation ignore-list. UnderPressure supplies pressure-insole contact annotations where permitted; the separate [footskate perception study](https://doi.org/10.2312/SCA/SCA11/287-294) found small slips detectable in most of its tested conditions, but used controlled mannequin stimuli and does not establish a universal CharForge threshold.

**Next measure.** E101/E111 freeze source-motion/subject/sequence IDs and near-duplicate groups before tuning; frames, views and seeds remain nested observations. Predeclare skeleton mapping, root alignment, time resampling and derivative filters. Use [UnderPressure](https://diglib.eg.org/items/def192e5-ad91-4409-b078-7d564fbaefb5) pressure-insole contacts as independent labels where permitted; contact truth is distinct from sole-height and slip proxies. [E122](experiments.json) preserves E029 history while re-auditing absolute slip speed and `slip_speed/v_gait` only at nonzero speed, and separately checks contact-proxy precision/recall. The browser's current `footAudit` still floors the speed denominator at 1 m/s, unlike the corrected Blender v2 implementation; bring both implementations into parity before using the playground audit. The perception paper can inform blinded boundary-rating samples, not set a universal percentage gate. AMASS-rendered clips remain a separate exact-GT condition. Only then judge E108's depth substitution.

### 8. Package, engine import, and runtime

**What is established.** README describes glTF and FBX packages, engine-specific map wiring, LODs, face shapes, and a manifest; the repo has a Blender asset audit and a three.js playground. `blender/package.py` exports multiple named FBX LOD meshes and a single-LOD glTF. The existing code comment/manifest overstates automatic LOD handling by Unreal/Godot; this was corrected to say engine grouping/recognition is unverified. Unreal's current FBX docs state one animation per skeletal-mesh file; Unity documents auto-creating an LOD Group from `_LODX` sibling meshes. No pinned cross-engine importer project was found.

**Gap.** A standards-valid GLB can still behave differently after an engine importer changes skin influences, material packing, animation grouping/sampling, morphs, or axis conversion. FBX has importer-specific details too. The phrase “game-ready” also needs a target runtime budget: package size, import time, load memory, and frame time on named hardware.

**Evidence and use.** The [Khronos glTF Validator](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator) checks assets against glTF 2.0 and writes machine-readable diagnostics; it does not emulate a game engine. [Unity model import docs](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/assets-and-media/asset-types/models/importing/importing-model-files), [Unreal's FBX skeletal-mesh docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/fbx-skeletal-mesh-pipeline-in-unreal-engine), and [Godot's import configuration docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html) each expose engine-specific behavior and version-sensitive options.

**Next measure.** E114 uses a format × engine-version × feature matrix on actual optimized `_web.glb` and release ZIP artifacts, not only full `out/` files. Report expected/imported clip coverage, normalized controller travel and heading error, complete manifest-driven locomotion/turn/capsule behavior, materials, morphs, spring-chain coverage and actual LOD selection. Record importer warnings, cold-load time, peak memory and frame time on named hardware. Separate “format valid,” “imports,” “plays correctly,” and “ships the feature” in the result.

### 9. Quality evaluation, repeatability, and rights

**What is established.** The living paper links code, measured data, tracker, and results; the README now distinguishes the Qwen-Image 2.1 non-commercial research/evaluation license from TRELLIS.2's project-material notice, and records the unresolved H3 fine-tune/base-license conflict. E023/E030 address local compute constraints and E106 plans a cost ablation.

**Gap.** The current paper build expands metric placeholders and validates citations/cross-references/media, but does not detect every hard-coded empirical number or require a number-to-artifact link. “Held-out” is not yet supported by frozen subject/sequence/prompt manifests, duplicate grouping, or an explicit rater protocol. There is also no per-run manifest of prompt/image rights, input/output hashes, dirty code state, stage settings/seeds, cache fingerprints and release ZIP digest. A release needs to distinguish *unknown* rights from permission, and benchmark scores from a legal basis for redistribution.

**Evidence and use.** MATE-3D's separate human-rated dimensions warn against an aggregate that can hide a missing feature behind attractive geometry. [Model Cards](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993) provide a structure for documenting model versions, intended uses, evaluation, and limitations; [Datasheets for Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010) provide one for data provenance, composition, collection, and recommended uses. Neither substitutes for reading actual model/data licences or legal review.

The source audit found concrete conditions that a generic “open-source” or MIT label would miss: Qwen-Image 2.1 permits non-commercial research/evaluation and requires a separate commercial license; the TRELLIS.2 project page says its provided materials are for academic/research use and not commercial exploitation; and MiniMax H3's agreement excludes the US, EU, UK and Republic of Korea, with its use policy restricting outputs outside the applicable territory. The H3 fine-tune actually named in the pipeline is labeled Apache-2.0, but its upstream agreement defines H3 Works to include model derivatives; compatibility remains unresolved. The pinned Mixamo mirror requires accepting access conditions including sharing contact information. Adobe's FAQ separately allows royalty-free use in projects, including commercial games, but does not by itself settle this mirror's redistribution terms. These are source-specific review gates, not legal conclusions. The repository also contains committed H3-generated demonstration media in `paper/media/` and `docs/paper/media/`; historical files were preserved and must be included in E115's rights/provenance inventory.

**Next measure.** [E121](experiments.json) adds a claim ledger plus a negative paper-build test, freezes evaluation split IDs and reports character/clip-clustered uncertainty and ICC model/type. E115 records exact per-run input-to-output lineage, dependency terms and unresolved fields; the manifest flags unknown rights rather than inferring permission and tests that changed input/config/code fingerprints invalidate cached stages. E106 reports peak memory as well as time/quality tradeoffs; E114 measures runtime costs. [E126](experiments.json) adds isolated-fixture UI/API acceptance for Studio queue/cancel/restart/log/lock transitions, queue order, duplicate/lost work and output continuity without perturbing active user work.

## Cross-cutting findings from the Luna Max audit

Six non-overlapping read-only lanes inspected prompt/generation, part labeling/retopo/UV/mips, deformation and motion, export/runtime, research validity, and the active E102 code. Their findings were reconciled against source files and primary references before entering the tracker.

- **E102 validity before interpretation.** `BVHTree.overlap()` supplies candidate index pairs; the current 3 cm rule is rest-centroid separation, not a triangle-distance test. E102 records broad-phase candidates only until a narrow phase and injected false-positive controls exist. Static source review confirms the audit asserts per-frame loop-triangle identity/order, preserves raw flags while adding sparse normal-ray exposure, and reports exact independent surface/BVH schedules. The pop quantity is a center-sample chord residual in mean-rest-edge units, with `n-2` eligible three-sample windows; it is cadence-specific displacement, not jerk. The code normalizes candidates by all possible triangle pairs, while candidate eligibility requires live triangles; it does not currently report an eligible-live-pair denominator. Add the planned step=1 / bvh-step=1 reference and injected one-frame defects to quantify missed-event rates before accepting a cheaper schedule. Current code can render per-measure worst samples, but that path also needs a runtime check. It does not exercise runtime face morphs or texture drift; E124/E120 remain distinct. Floor flags remain vertices below a fixed plane, not contact-conditioned foot penetration. The normal-ray subset is neither camera visibility nor pixel coverage. Existing outputs refuse replacement by default; `--force` is explicit. No Blender job was run, so safeguards and render/output behavior remain unvalidated at runtime.
  - *Update 2026-09-30 (runtime validation).* The controlled cases now exist and run (`tools/aberration_controls.py`, results in `research/data/e102_controls.json`): 22/22 checks pass under DQS and LBS, including 0.5/1/2/5 cm penetrations measured exactly and a one-frame pop found at step 1. `BVHTree.overlap()` was tested directly: two triangles whose boxes overlap without touching are not returned, so it is a triangle-triangle test and the pairs are real intersections (renamed `intersecting_pairs`); contact vs penetration is separated by a measured depth (`penetration_*`). The controls caught three errors that are now fixed: quads re-split by pose (triangles now fixed at rest; the loop-triangle guard was right to trip), depth capped by a ring search, and false "inside" readings at open region rims and between layers (straight-projection plus far-wall ray test). `--step 1` is now the default. Human calibration remains open.
- **E029 denominator and audit parity.** `blender/foot_audit.py` v2 now reports absolute slip m/s and ratio only against actual nonzero speed, and collector/table helpers exclude legacy sub-1 m/s ratios. The browser `web/playground.html` `footAudit` still uses `Math.max(speed,1)`, so its sub-1 m/s ratio remains understated. Its contact sample also uses consecutive grounded ticks and tracks the same sole vertex, while Blender uses a centered finite difference at the current lowest vertex; harmonize both under fixed tests or report them as different estimands. Old files and numbers were not overwritten; E029 is reopened pending a same-schedule rerun and recollection. The geometric 4 mm contact proxy also still needs separate precision/recall against permitted pressure-insole labels.
- **Description support contract.** E112 now tests both supported attributes and deliberate unsupported requests (props/held objects, pose/action, numeric height), including short prompts near the word-count boundary. Attribute loss should be attributed to its first pipeline stage; ignored input is not “faithful.”
- **THUman validity and rights.** E113 now says synthetic rendered orbits, not captured calibrated views, and requires exact release pinning plus written permission before third-party sharing of derived files. Without permission the study remains internal.
- **Export claims.** Unreal/Unity LOD and animation rules are version-sensitive. `blender/package.py` no longer claims Godot/Unreal automatically generate LODs; it says FBX has named decimated meshes and glTF has LOD0, with actual runtime acceptance deferred to E114. The optimized web asset and release ZIP are the shipping boundary.
- **Evidence integrity.** The reproducibility section now describes the build's checks accurately: it does not prove hand-entered numbers are tied to artifacts. E121 freezes groups and adds claim lineage/negative tests. Benchmark frames/views/seeds are nested, not independent observations; blind ratings need boundary examples and reported ICC semantics.

## Parallel work packets and dependencies

The large follow-up is compartmentalized by artifact ownership so Luna Max reviewers/workers can work concurrently without colliding. Only one packet may run a heavy Blender/GPU job at a time under `.claude/skills/charforge/SKILL.md`; all parallel agents must use read-only review until one measurement owner holds the GPU lock. Each packet must update its own experiment entry, raw artifact, result/decision, and relevant paper section as one evidence chain.

| Packet | Owns | Depends on | Parallel / gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — motion/measurements | E102, E029/E122, E101/E111; `blender/aberration_audit.py`, `blender/foot_audit.py`, motion result schemas | Runbook; frozen frames/splits; active Studio/GPU lock released by its owner | E102 and E122 can be reviewed in parallel; Blender measurement runs serialized |
| B — prompt/scan fidelity | E112–E113; `pipeline/describe.py`, prompt ledgers, private scan protocol | Dataset/model versions, permissions, held-out prompt/scan manifest | Independent of mesh-quality coding; no sharing THUman derivatives without permission |
| C — geometry/parts | E116–E118; `pipeline/solidify.py`, `pipeline/parts.py`, `blender/retopo.py` | Gold-set labels and fixed source/output character IDs | Parallel static metric design; all Blender runs serialized with Packet A |
| D — texture | E119–E120; `tools/asset_audit.py`, `pipeline/project_texture.py`, runtime material fixture | Stable retopo/UV and pinned texture compression/sampler | Metric and fixture design parallel; runtime renders serialized |
| E — hands/face/layers | E123–E125; hand/face rig files and E110 output boundaries | Licensed held-out data; E110 independent layers before E125 | Hand/face protocol parallel; E125 waits for E110 and any cloth run remains serial |
| F — product/reproducibility | E114–E115/E121/E126; package manifest, claim ledger, engine/Studio smoke fixtures | Exact release artifacts; pinned engine versions; disposable Studio fixture | Claim ledger and fixture review parallel; importer/build tests isolated from active Studio |

**Suggested sequence:** finish static E102/E029 code review first, then run the controlled E102 and E122 measurement batches when the GPU/Studio lock is available. Freeze split/artifact schemas (E121) before E101/E111/E112/E113 rating or benchmarking. Packets B/C/D/E/F can prepare protocols and non-GPU fixtures in parallel, but do not publish scores until the shared split, rights and provenance manifests are fixed. The following year-long research goal is considered well specified once each pipeline stage has a named owner, executable method, defined truth/proxy/human boundary, artifact path and dependency gate—not when every planned experiment is marked done.

## Suggested development order

1. **Close measure integrity:** E102 controlled cases, E122 corrected foot ratio, E101 synthetic truth, E111 subject-held-out footage and E121 claim/split ledger.
2. **Validate asset stages:** E116/E117 topology and labels, E118/E119/E120 surface/UV/runtime texture, then E112/E113 prompt and scan studies after rights/splits are frozen.
3. **Prove delivery:** E114 format × engine × feature matrix, E115 per-run lineage/licence fields and E126 isolated Studio acceptance.
4. **Use stronger truth on visible failure areas:** E123/E124 hands and faces; E110 then E125 layered secondary motion; continue E026/E105/E107/E109.
5. **Compare alternatives only after protocols stabilize:** E103 rig baselines, E104 generators, E108 depth and E106 time/quality ablations.

## Reading list added to the paper bibliography

- Prompt/reference fidelity: [TIFA (ICCV 2023)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Hu_TIFA_Accurate_and_Interpretable_Text-to-Image_Faithfulness_Evaluation_with_Question_Answering_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf); [GenEval (NeurIPS 2023)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/a3bf71c7c63f0c3bcb7ff67c67b1e7b1-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html).
- Text-to-3D quality and human judgment: [MATE-3D (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zhang_Benchmarking_and_Learning_Multi-Dimensional_Quality_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf); [LPIPS (CVPR 2018)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Zhang_The_Unreasonable_Effectiveness_CVPR_2018_paper.html).
- Scan-anchored geometry and appearance: [THUman 2.0 dataset and terms](https://github.com/ytrock/THuman2.0-Dataset).
- Motion ground truth and stress sets: [AMASS (ICCV 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03278); [TotalCapture (BMVC 2017)](https://cvssp.org/projects/totalcapture/TotalCapture/TrumbleBMVC2017.pdf); [3DPW (ECCV 2018)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ECCV_2018/papers/Timo_von_Marcard_Recovering_Accurate_3D_ECCV_2018_paper.pdf); [AIST++ (ICCV 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08779) and its [annotation/split documentation](https://google.github.io/aistplusplus_dataset/).
- Expressive/dynamic quality: [Pose Space Deformation (SIGGRAPH 2000)](https://doi.org/10.1145/344779.344862); [MANO (SIGGRAPH Asia 2017)](https://mano.is.tue.mpg.de/); [SMPL-X (CVPR 2019)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Pavlakos_Expressive_Body_Capture_3D_Hands_Face_and_Body_From_a_Single_Image_CVPR_2019_paper.html); [AU-Blendshape (ICCV 2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Li_AU-Blendshape_for_Fine-grained_Stylized_3D_Facial_Expression_Manipulation_ICCV_2025_paper.html); [DDH-QA (ICME 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12734); [DHQA-4D (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2026.114567).
- Foot contact and perception: [UnderPressure (CGF 2022)](https://diglib.eg.org/items/def192e5-ad91-4409-b078-7d564fbaefb5); [Perceptual evaluation of footskate cleanup (SCA 2011)](https://doi.org/10.2312/SCA/SCA11/287-294). The latter informs blinded rating design; its mannequin-study thresholds are not a universal acceptance rule.
- Geometry/parts/UV: [ManifoldPlus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11621); [Metro](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8659.00236); [semantic segmentation calibration](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Wang_On_Calibrating_Semantic_Segmentation_Models_Analyses_and_an_Algorithm_CVPR_2023_paper.html); [Texture Mapping Progressive Meshes](https://hhoppe.com/proj/tmpm/).
- Hands, faces, and garments: [InterHand2.6M](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09309); [D3DFACS](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2011.6126510); [TEMPEH/FaMoS](https://tempeh.is.tue.mpg.de/); [CAPE](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Ma_Learning_to_Dress_3D_People_in_Generative_Clothing_CVPR_2020_paper.html); [CLOTH3D++](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v133/madadi21a.html); [UnderPressure](https://diglib.eg.org/items/def192e5-ad91-4409-b078-7d564fbaefb5).
- Runtime sampling and provenance: official [Unity mipmap documentation](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.5/manual/materials-and-shaders/textures/textures-getting-started/texture-mipmaps-introduction); [W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [CycloneDX ML-BOM](https://www.cyclonedx.org/capabilities/mlbom/); [Ten Simple Rules for Reproducible Computational Research](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003285); [Khronos glTF Validator](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator); official [Unity](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/assets-and-media/asset-types/models/importing/importing-model-files), [Unreal](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/fbx-skeletal-mesh-pipeline-in-unreal-engine), and [Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html) importer docs.
